👋Context

Now you have completed your high level user journey, let’s jump into the low level specifics and address the task flow. A task flow is a step-by-step diagram showing the specific actions a user takes to complete one primary task within your product. Unlike user journeys (which cover broader emotional or contextual paths), task flows zoom in on the logical flow of screens and decisions. They are essentially instructional.

The best way to think about making a task flow is just imagine that your mom called you and asked you to help her download instagram onto her phone. We would just give her the steps, making things as straight forward as possible for her. Nothing more, nothing less.

Always stay goal-orientated. A task flow exists to get the user to one goal, so state that goal before you draw anything, and write it down as the final step of the flow. If a step doesn't move the user towards the goal, it doesn't belong.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • State the user's goal in one sentence before you start.
  • Create a clear task flow diagram for your main use case, ending on that goal.
  • Keep every step moving the user towards the goal.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. State the goal. Write one sentence: what is the user trying to achieve? For example: "Mum has Instagram installed and is logged in."
  2. Revisit your user journey
  3. Break the task down into the minimal steps a user must take.
  4. Use simple a flowchart like arrows and a few boxes. Nothing fancy.
  5. Connect steps with arrows to represent the sequence.
  6. Keep the flow linear and focused on a single goal (avoid branching into multiple tasks).
  7. Write the goal as the final step. The last box is the goal you stated in step 1, not "End".
Extra Notes
  • There should always be a verb in your step like tap, press, swipe, unlock, create, add, delete
  • Avoid too much detail (keep it readable).
  • Do the minimum steps
  • Test your flow by walking through it with a friend to see if it is straight forward.
Template

Unlock phone 👉 Open App Store 👉 Tap search bar 👉 Type “Instagram” 👉 Select Instagram from results 👉 Tap “Get” 👉 Wait for download 👉 Tap “Open” 👉 Log in 👉 🎯 Goal: Mum has Instagram installed and is logged in

Tips
  • State the goal first, and write it as the final step. Then fill in the steps in between.
  • If a step doesn't move the user towards the goal, cut it.
  • Use verbs in action steps (“Select item,” “Enter details”).
  • Stick to one task flow per diagram.

Summary

Task flows are a quick way to map the logic and sequence of a user’s actions. By focusing on one primary task, students gain clarity on what screens, inputs, and decisions need to exist. This exercise ensures designs are user-centered, efficient, and free from unnecessary complexity before moving into wireframes.
