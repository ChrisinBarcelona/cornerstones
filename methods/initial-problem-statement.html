👋Context

Every sprint has to start somewhere. Before you've spoken to a single user, you already have a hunch about what the problem is: from the brief, from your own experience or from something you've noticed. The initial problem statement writes that hunch down as one clear sentence.

It is deliberately undefined: it is an assumption, not a finding. There is no data behind it yet, and that's fine. Its job is to give your discovery a starting point and a focus, so your research goal, research questions and interviews all pull in the same direction.

By the end of Discovery, your research will have confirmed it, changed it or thrown it out completely. In Define, you write the Validated Problem Statement, the version that is backed by evidence. Comparing the two is one of the most useful stories in your case study: it shows what your research actually discovered.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • Write down your starting assumption about the problem in one clear sentence.
  • Separate what you believe from what you know.
  • Use the statement to focus your research goal and research questions.
  • Be ready to change it when the research says otherwise.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Read the brief. Underline who it is about and what seems to be going wrong.
  2. Write your hunch. Use the template: "We believe that [user] struggles to [task or need] because [our assumption]." - Example: "We believe that tourists at Barceloneta struggle to swim without worrying about their belongings, because there is nowhere safe to leave them on the beach."
  3. Label it. Write UNPROVEN next to it. Every part of it is an assumption.
  4. List your assumptions. Underline each assumption in the sentence (who, what, why) and list them. These are the things your research must check.
  5. Turn them into research. Each assumption becomes a research question. "Is it really tourists?" "Is it really their belongings that stop them?"
  6. Pin it up. Keep the statement visible for the whole of Discovery, then compare it with your Validated Problem Statement in Define.
Template
  • 🟡 Initial problem statement (unproven): We believe that [user] struggles to [task or need] because [assumption].
  • ❓ Assumptions to test: 1… 2… 3…
  • 🔍 Research questions they lead to: 1… 2… 3…
Tips
  • Keep it short: one sentence, no solutions, no features.
  • Be honest that it's a guess. "We believe" keeps everyone humble.
  • The more specific your assumption, the easier it is to test.
  • Being wrong is a good result. A discovery that changes nothing probably didn't discover anything.
AI Assist

Give Claude your initial problem statement and ask: "List every assumption hidden in this sentence, and suggest one research question to test each." Then decide yourself which assumptions matter most.
