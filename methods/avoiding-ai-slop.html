👋Context

AI slop is generic, AI-generated work that could belong to anyone: the same purple gradient, the same centred hero with three feature cards, the same emoji icons, placeholder-sounding copy and a font chosen because it was the default. It's the single biggest reason AI-assisted portfolios get rejected. Reviewers spot it in seconds, and it tells them the designer didn't make the decisions.

AI produces slop when it has nothing specific to work from, so it falls back on the average of everything it has seen. The cure is to give it specifics up front (rules) and to judge the result against a standard afterwards (a quality rubric). Both are human jobs.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • Recognise the signs of generic AI design.
  • Write rules that give AI your brand, content and constraints before it starts.
  • Score AI output with a quality rubric, and fix what fails.
  • Show in your case study which decisions were yours.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Spot the slop. Ask an AI tool for "a landing page for a beach locker app" with no other context. List every generic choice you see.
  2. Write your rules. For your own project, write the rules the AI must follow: brand colours and type (from your style guide), real copy, your grid and spacing, your components, accessibility requirements, and a short list of things it must never do.
  3. Add references. Attach your mood board, style guide or a screen you've already designed.
  4. Generate again with your rules and references.
  5. Score it with the rubric below, 1–5 per row.
  6. Fix it yourself. Anything that scores 3 or less, you redesign: don't just re-roll the prompt.
  7. Before and after. Put the generic version, the AI-with-rules version and your final version side by side for your case study.
Extra Notes

Common signs of AI slop: default fonts and purple–blue gradients; a centred hero plus three cards; emoji used as icons; vague copy ("Unlock your potential", "Seamless experience"); stock-photo people; everything the same size and weight; decoration with no purpose.

Template
Rubric (score 1–5) What a 5 looks like
Specific Could only belong to this product and these users
On brand Uses your colours, type, imagery and tone of voice
Real content Real copy and data from your research, no filler
Hierarchy The most important thing is obvious at a glance
System Uses your grid, spacing and components consistently
Accessible Passes contrast checks, clear labels, readable sizes
Purposeful Every element answers a user need from your research
Tips
  • Rules in, quality out: the more specific your brief, the less generic the result.
  • Keep a "never" list and add to it every time you spot a new cliché.
  • Judge with the rubric, not with "it looks nice".
  • The final design decisions must be yours, and your case study should show them.
AI Assist

Ask Claude to act as a strict reviewer: paste your rubric and a screenshot, and ask it to score each row and point out anything generic. Then make the fixes yourself.

Summary

AI slop is generic work produced when AI has nothing specific to go on. Prevent it with rules (your brand, content, system and a "never" list) and catch it with a quality rubric. The AI drafts; you judge, fix and own the final design.
