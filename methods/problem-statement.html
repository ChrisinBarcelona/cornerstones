👋Context

Let’s begin the formative part of our sprint with a defined problem statement that’s built on the solid foundation of our earlier research. A problem statement is a clear, concise description of the main issue or challenge that users face.

Hopefully, by now, you will have learned much more about our user behaviour and as a result, our early assumption has been challenged enough that you can create an informed statement which summarises the problem and perhaps, because of our user interviews, even why it’s happening.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

By the end of this exercise, students will be able to:

  • Identify and articulate user pain points based on researched insights
  • Frame a clear, concise, and actionable problem statement
  • Communicate the problem effectively to team members and stakeholders

🛠️Main Exercise

Step 1: Review Research Insights

  • Look back at user interviews, surveys, or observations from earlier research.

Step 2: Identify Core Problems

  • Review research notes.
  • Ask “Why?” repeatedly to get to the root of each pain point.
  • Pick one main user problem to focus on.

Step 3: Draft Problem Statement

  • Fill in the template: "[User] needs a way to [need] because [insight from research]."
  • Example: "Tourists swimming at Barceloneta in peak season need a way to keep their phone and wallet safe while they're in the water, because they currently take turns to swim or stay out of the sea."

Step 4: Check it

  • Is it written from the user's point of view?
  • Is it free of solutions?
  • Can you point to the research behind every part of it?
  • Share it with your team and refine it together.
Tips
  • Write from the user's point of view, in their words.
  • Keep the solution out of it: no apps, features or buttons.
  • Be specific about who and when: "tourists swimming at Barceloneta in July", not "people".
  • Every claim should trace back to a research finding.
  • One problem, one statement. If you have three, pick the one that matters most.

Checklist

☐ Reviewed all your research insights

☐ Found the root cause of the main pain point with repeated "why?"

☐ Wrote one problem statement using the template

☐ Checked it has no solution in it and is backed by evidence

Summary

A problem statement is one clear, concise sentence that names who has the problem, what it is, when it happens and why it matters, all grounded in research and free of solutions. It becomes the anchor for every idea and decision in the rest of the sprint.
