👋Context

Designers always have ideas early, often before the research has even started. Ignoring them doesn't work; they sit in the back of your mind and quietly bias everything you hear. A better approach is to get them out of your head and onto the wall as a solution hypothesis: a short statement of what you think will solve the problem, and how you'll know.

A hypothesis is unproven. It is not a design decision, and it is not a feature list. It is a bet that needs evidence before it becomes anything more. Writing it down early makes your assumptions visible, so your research can test them instead of just confirming them.

Jeff Gothelf's Lean UX approach puts it simply: treat every design as a hypothesis, and every release as an experiment.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • Write your early solution idea as a testable hypothesis.
  • Name the outcome that would prove it right, and the signal that would prove it wrong.
  • Keep the hypothesis separate from the evidence, so research isn't biased by it.
  • Revisit it after Discovery and decide: keep, change or drop.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Start from your initial problem statement. The hypothesis should answer it.
  2. Write the hypothesis. Use the template: "We believe that [solution] for [user] will achieve [outcome]. We'll know we're right when we see [measurable signal]." - Example: "We believe that waterproof lockers by the lifeguard towers for tourists at Barceloneta will let them swim without worrying. We'll know we're right when at least 6 out of 10 tourists we ask say they'd pay €3 to use one."
  3. Label it. Write HYPOTHESIS: UNPROVEN next to it.
  4. Name the riskiest assumption. What must be true for this to work? Circle it.
  5. Plan the test. Which research method (interview, survey, desk research, site visit) will give you evidence for or against it?
  6. Revisit it. At the end of Discovery, mark it supported, changed or dropped, with one line of evidence.
Template
  • 💡 We believe that [solution] for [user] will achieve [outcome].
  • 📏 We'll know we're right when we see [measurable signal].
  • ⚠️ Riskiest assumption: …
  • 🔍 How we'll test it: …
  • ✅ / ✏️ / ❌ After Discovery: supported / changed / dropped, because…
Tips
  • One hypothesis per card. If you have five ideas, write five hypotheses.
  • Make the signal measurable: a number, a behaviour, a quote you'd expect to hear.
  • Look for evidence that proves you wrong, not just evidence that proves you right.
  • A dropped hypothesis is a success: you saved yourself from building the wrong thing.

Summary

A solution hypothesis is your early idea written as a testable bet: what you'll build, for whom, what it should achieve and how you'll know. It stays unproven until research gives you evidence, and dropping it is as valuable as keeping it.
