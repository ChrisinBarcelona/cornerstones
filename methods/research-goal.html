All research is simply an extension of what you want to know.

👋Context

Before diving into sources or data, you need clarity on why you are researching and what you hope to learn. This follows the scientific method that we apply to UX.

Research Goal Research Questions Survey / Interview Questions
Broad and high-level aim what you want to achieve or learn. Specific, open-ended questions that guide your study and help you answer the research goal. The actual participant-facing questions you use to collect data to answer your research questions.
Goals are why you’re doing research. Research Questions are what you need to learn to meet those goals. Survey/Interview Questions are how you get that information from participants.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

By the end of this activity, you will have a clear and written statement of your research goal(s) that will guide the rest of your project.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Write down a big question you are genuinely curious about.
  2. Break it down: What smaller questions branch off from it?
  3. Reframe it as a goal: What do you want to understand, explain, or discover by the end of your research?
  4. Share your research goal with a partner or in a small group for feedback.
Extra Notes
  • Keep your research goals broad enough to explore, but narrow enough to stay focused.
  • It should connect to your interests, community, or a problem you think matters.
Template
  • Big question: ______
  • Smaller questions: ______
  • My research goal is: ______
Tips
  • Think about what you would still care about learning even if it’s not for school.
  • If you can explain your goal to a friend in one sentence, you’re on track.
AI Assist

You can ask an AI tool:

  • “Help me brainstorm smaller research questions about [topic].”
  • “Rewrite my research goal so it’s clearer.”

🎥Media

Resources

  • Sentence starters:
    • “I want to understand why/how…”
    • “My research will help explain…”
    • “I hope to discover whether…”
Checklist

☐ Wrote down a big question you're curious about

☐ Broke it into smaller questions

☐ Reframed it as one research goal (understand, explain or discover)

☐ Got feedback from a partner

Summary

Your research goal is your compass. It shows you where you want to go, keeps you from getting lost in random information, and ensures your final product has direction and purpose.
