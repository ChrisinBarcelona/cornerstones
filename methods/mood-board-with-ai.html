👋Context

A mood board captures the feeling a product should create, in images, colour, type and words. Nielsen Norman Group's process starts with 4–5 mood words, then collects and arranges visuals around them, and finishes with feedback.

AI image tools change the "collect" step. Instead of only searching for images, you can generate them from your mood words, look at what comes back, react, and generate again. This is a multimodal visual loop: words become images, images become new words, and the direction gets sharper with every round.

The risk is the same as with all AI work: it is fast, and it is generic. AI images drift towards the same glossy look, and they can't know your users. You stay the art director: the mood words come from your research, and every image on the final board has to earn its place.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • Use mood words from your research to drive AI image generation.
  • Run at least three generate–react–refine loops and see the direction sharpen.
  • Combine generated and found images into one coherent mood board.
  • Explain why each image is on the board.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Mood words (5 min). Write 4–5 mood words from your research and persona, e.g. "calm, trustworthy, local, warm".
  2. Loop 1: Generate. In an AI image tool, prompt with your mood words plus the subject: "Interior of a calm, warm neighbourhood pharmacy, soft morning light, muted greens." Generate 4–8 images.
  3. React. Mark each image: 🟢 keep, 🔴 avoid. Write why in a word or two ("too clinical", "right warmth").
  4. Loop 2 and 3: Refine. Rewrite the prompt using your reactions ("less clinical, more wood and plants"). Try a different modality too: upload a photo you like, or ask for a colour palette, textures or a type pairing in the same mood.
  5. Assemble the board. On one FigJam or Figma frame, combine your best generated images with real photos, colour swatches, type samples and the mood words. Give the most important visuals the most space.
  6. Share and get feedback. Classmates add green and red sticky notes. Finalise.
Template
  • 🗝️ Mood words
  • 🔁 Loop log: prompt → images → reactions, ×3
  • 🎨 Colours and textures
  • 🔠 Type samples
  • 🖼️ Final board
Tips
  • Keep the loop log. Showing how the prompts evolved is great case-study material.
  • Be specific about light, materials, colour and setting; vague prompts give stock-photo results.
  • Avoid mixing moods. Calm and serious don't sit well next to cute and loud.
  • Don't fill the board with interface screenshots; that's a competitive analysis, not a mood board.
  • Check the tool's licence before using images in public work, and say which images are AI-generated.
AI Assist

Ask Claude to look at your board and answer: "Which three images don't match my mood words, and why?" Then decide whether you agree.

Summary

Mood boarding with AI turns the "collect visuals" step into a loop: generate from your mood words, react, refine and repeat. Used well, it explores more directions faster; the designer still chooses the words, judges every image and builds a board that fits the users.

Nielsen Norman Group has no video on mood boards themselves; the videos here cover judging how users react to a visual direction and choosing colour.
