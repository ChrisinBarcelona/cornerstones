👋Context

Now that you’ve set a research goal, the next step is asking the right questions. If your goal is the destination, then your questions are the map — they guide you step by step toward understanding.

Let’s check the chart again …

Research Goal Research Questions Survey / Interview Questions
Broad, high-level aim what you want to achieve or learn. Specific, open-ended questions that guide your study and help you answer the research goal. The actual participant-facing questions you use to collect data to answer your research questions.
Goals are why you’re doing research. Research Questions are what you need to learn to meet those goals. Survey/Interview Questions are how you get that information from participants.

🎯Goal / Learning outcome

By the end of this activity, you will develop strong research questions that connect directly to your research goal and can guide your investigation.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Re-read the research goal you wrote in the first exercise.
  2. Imagine you are a detective: What clues (questions) would you need to ask in order to solve the “mystery” of your research goal?
  3. Write 3–5 research questions that break your goal into smaller, answerable parts.
    • make thAt least one should be descriptive (what/when/where).
    • At least one should be analytical (why/how).
    • At least one should be evaluative (so what/what if).
  4. Share with a peer: Do your questions feel connected to your goal, or do they drift off-topic?
Extra Notes
  • A good research question is not something you can answer with just a yes/no.
  • Strong questions often start with why or how.
Template
  • My research goal: ______
  • My research questions:
Tips
  • Think of your questions as puzzle pieces. Together, they should form a picture that answers your research goal.
  • Don’t be afraid to draft too many — you can refine later.
AI Assist

You can ask an AI tool:

  • “Here’s my research goal: ___. Suggest some related research questions.”
  • “Which of these questions are too broad or too narrow?”
Checklist
  • [ ] I reviewed my research goal.
  • [ ] I drafted at least 3–5 questions.
  • [ ] I included different types (descriptive, analytical, evaluative).
  • [ ] My questions connect back to my research goal.
Summary

Your research goal tells you where you’re going. Your research questions tell you how to get there. Together, they make sure your project has both purpose and direction.
