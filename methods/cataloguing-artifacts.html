👋Context

A case study is only as good as the evidence behind it. Nielsen Norman Group's advice on UX portfolios is that hiring managers want to see how you work, not just your final screens. That means early sketches, whiteboard photos, research notes, maps, test results and iterations.

The problem: after four sprints, that evidence is scattered across FigJam boards, Figma files, phones, notebooks and group chats. Before you can write a single case study, you need to find it, sort it and choose.

NN/g's first step to a portfolio is to take inventory of all your projects, then pick the 3–5 that best show the work you want to do next. This exercise does exactly that, for every project you've done on the course.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • Collect every artifact from all your projects into one organised place.
  • Label each artifact with its project, method, stage and date.
  • Choose the projects, and the artifacts, that best show your process and strengths.
  • Set up a habit of saving artifacts as you go.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Make one home. Create a Figma (or FigJam) file called "Portfolio Archive" with one page per project.
  2. Hunt and gather. Go through each project and pull in everything: briefs, research questions, survey results, interview notes, site-visit photos, personas, journey maps, blueprints, affinity maps, sketches, wireframes, prototypes, test notes, before/after screens, retrospectives.
  3. Label each one. Under every artifact, write: project · method · stage · date · what it shows.
  4. Sort by the case-study outline. Group each project's artifacts under: the problem, your role, research, ideas (including abandoned ones), the solution, testing and impact, and what you learned.
  5. Take inventory. For each project answer: Which skills does it show best? What did I enjoy? Where did I add the most value? What did I learn? Is there a good story?
  6. Choose. Pick the 3–5 projects that best match the job you want, and star the 5–8 strongest artifacts in each. Note any gaps (e.g. "no photo of the paper sketches") and fill them now while you can.
Template

For each artifact:

  • 🗂️ Project
  • 🛠️ Method
  • 🪜 Stage
  • 📅 Date
  • 💡 What it shows
Tips
  • Keep the messy stuff. Crumpled sketches and sticky-note walls prove the process.
  • Photograph physical work in good light, straight on.
  • Check permissions: blur participants' faces and names, and ask before using client or team material.
  • Note who did what on team projects, so you can describe your role honestly.
  • From now on, save artifacts at the end of every day, not at the end of the project.

Summary

Cataloguing artifacts is the groundwork of every case study. Collecting, labelling and sorting everything you made lets you see your work as a whole, choose the projects that show you best, and back every claim in your case study with real evidence of your process.
