👋Context

Synthesis is where research turns into insight: making sense of everything you collected, across all your sources. It is slow, careful work, and AI tools can genuinely speed up parts of it: transcribing, summarising, suggesting codes and grouping notes.

But Nielsen Norman Group is clear about the limits. AI can misread, miss or invent insights. Its groupings are often based on surface keywords, with a large "Other" pile. It can't watch what users do in a usability test, and it can't tell when a participant was embarrassed, primed by the interviewer or not really your target user. Their advice: use AI as an assistant for the first pass, and never outsource the analysis itself.

In this exercise you use AI to speed up synthesis of your usability-testing notes, then check every result against the raw data.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • Use AI to transcribe, summarise and suggest codes for research data.
  • Protect participants' privacy before sharing any data with an AI tool.
  • Verify AI output against the raw data and correct it.
  • Write themes that you can defend with evidence.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. Clean the data. Gather your notes and transcripts. Remove names and personal details (P1, P2… instead of names) before pasting anything into a chatbot.
  2. Check transcripts. If AI transcribed your sessions, spot-check them against the recordings, especially with several speakers or accents.
  3. Give AI the context. Paste in your research questions and two or three examples of good codes, e.g. "Unclear next step", "Trusts reviews".
  4. Ask for a first pass. Prompt: "Here are my research questions and anonymised notes from 5 usability tests. Suggest up to 10 codes, and for each one quote the notes that support it with the participant number."
  5. Verify. For every code, go back to the notes. Delete the ones that aren't supported, merge duplicates, and add anything it missed. Pay special attention to what users did, not just what they said.
  6. Write the themes yourself. Group your checked codes into 3–5 themes, each with a one-sentence insight and two pieces of evidence.
  7. Share the source. Mark which parts were AI-suggested and which you changed.
Tips
  • Garbage in, garbage out. Clear, complete notes get far better suggestions.
  • Ask for quotes and participant numbers every time, so you can check the evidence.
  • Be suspicious of neat answers. If a theme has no supporting quote, it doesn't exist.
  • AI is good at "what was said". You are needed for "what it means".
  • Check your school's or client's rules before uploading any research data.
AI Assist

Good follow-up prompts:

  • "Which of these codes has the weakest evidence?"
  • "Find notes that contradict this theme."
  • "Which notes did you put in 'Other', and why?"

Summary

AI can make synthesis faster by taking the first pass: transcripts, summaries, suggested codes and rough groups. But it can misread and invent, and it can't see behaviour. The designer's job is to protect participants' data, check every suggestion against the evidence, and write the final themes themselves.
