👋Context

By now you have more ideas and features than you can possibly build. You need a fair way to choose.

The Desirability, Viability, Feasibility (DVF) scorecard was developed by IDEO in the early 2000s and is still widely used across the industry. Nielsen Norman Group describes it as a prioritisation method that scores each idea against three criteria and adds up the totals:

  • Desirability: Do users actually want or need this? What unique value does it give them?
  • Viability: Is it good for the business? What does it cost, and is it sustainable over time?
  • Feasibility: Can we build it? Do the skills, time and technology exist?

The best ideas sit where all three overlap. The score is a starting point for discussion, not the final answer.

🎯Goal / Learning Outcome

  • Score a list of ideas or features for desirability, viability and feasibility.
  • Back each score with evidence from research, the business model or the team.
  • Rank ideas and discuss the results as a team.
  • Agree which ideas go into the next prototype.

🛠️Main Exercise

  1. List the ideas. Take 5–10 ideas or features from your ideation or affinity map. Write each as a short phrase.
  2. Build the scorecard. A table with one row per idea and columns for Desirability, Viability, Feasibility and Total.
  3. Score 1–10. For each idea, score each criterion and write one line of evidence for the score (a research finding, a cost, a skill you do or don't have).
  4. Add up and rank. Sort from highest to lowest total.
  5. Discuss and adjust. Does the ranking feel right? Move items if the team can justify it. If one criterion matters more for your project, weight it (e.g. desirability ×2).
  6. Decide. Choose the top ideas to take into your prototype, and note which ones you are parking.

Tips
  • Score as informed as possible. Bring your research, your Business Model Canvas and someone who knows the tech.
  • Score each criterion across all ideas before moving to the next. It keeps the scale consistent.
  • A low feasibility score isn't always a "no": maybe it's "not yet".
  • Don't let the maths decide for you. The conversation is the point.

Summary

DVF analysis helps a team choose between ideas fairly. Scoring each idea for what users want, what the business needs and what can be built, then discussing the ranking, turns a long wish list into a short, defensible plan.
