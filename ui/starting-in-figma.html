So to go over the absolute basics, we are going to do a simple exercise on how to make an animated button. This exercise covers about 99.9% of everything you would actually need to know in order to create a full prototype in Figma, so if your an absolute beginner, let’s get started!

In this tutorial you will learn how to:

  1. Make An Account
  2. Create A Canvas
  3. Name A Frame
  4. Copy A Frame
  5. Add Fill Colour
  6. Add Shape Layer
  7. Give Shape A Name
  8. Align Shape to Centre
  9. Add Fill Colour to Shape Layer
  10. Add Stroke to Shape
  11. Change Stroke Colour on Shape
  12. Increase Stroke Thickness on Shape
  13. Add Shadow to Shape
  14. Change Shape Radius
  15. Copy Shape Layer
  16. Change to Prototype Mode
  17. Create Connections between Frames
  18. Activate Smart Animation
  19. Repeat the Process
  20. Play Prototype

Let’s Go!

1. Make a Figma Account

Navigate to https://www.figma.com and create an account.

Click on New Design File

Now you should see an Interface that looks something like this; the example below.

Now it’s time to make your first frame. On your keyboard press;

  • Make Frame: F

You’ll see a panel has opened up on the right hand side;

2. Select Canvas: iPhone 14

For this demonstration we will be using an iPhone 14. For more information on choosing the right frame see here

Congratulations! You have now created your very first frame and used your first keyboard shortcut! Well done!

That’s a little big! Let’s Zoom Out!

Zoom Out:

On Windows:

  • Zoom In: Ctrl + Plus sign (+)
  • Zoom Out: Ctrl + Minus sign (-)

On Mac:

  • Zoom In: Cmd + Plus sign (+)
  • Zoom Out: Cmd + Minus sign (-)

That’s better! Now we can see. Let’s give your frame a new name by clicking on the title.

3. Name Your Frame

You’ll notice on the left hand side the, the file name has changed too.

Now we should copy the frame; this is a popular shortcut and you will probably be familiar with it.

4. Copy Your Frame

Click your new label ‘My First Frame’ and copy it.

On Windows:

  • Copy: Ctrl + C
  • Paste: Ctrl + V

On Mac:

  • Copy: Cmd + C
  • Paste: Cmd + V

Great, let’s name the new frame ‘My Second Frame’

Click off canvas to de-activate the selected frame.

5. Add Fill Colour

Now we are going to add colour I’ll be using two colours only: their Hex Values are

Beige: #F2C0A2

Maroon: #F24738

Activate your first frame. remember, just click the label ‘My First Frame’.

Navigate to the right hand sidebar to Fill.

Click the white thumbnail beside FFFFFF to open the colour palette.

Change FFFFFF to Beige: #F2C0A2

Repeat the process for your second frame but this time; change FFFFFF to Maroon: #F24738

Click off canvas to close the colour palette.

6. Add Shape Layer

Now we are going to make some awesome shapes and later on, give them some cool motion animation so let’s get started!

Activate ‘My First Frame’

Now we are going to;

  • Make a Rectangle: R

and increase the size to 150px x 150px;

by clicking on the edge and scaling up;

Scale out: shift + drag out

Scale in: shift + drag in

7. Give Your Shape a Name

Now let’s change the name to ‘My First Button’

8. Align Shape to Centre

and align shape to centre of the frame;

On Windows:

  • Align vertical centres: Alt + V
  • Align horizontal centres: Alt + H

On Mac:

  • Align vertical centres: Option ⌥ + V
  • Align horizontal centres: Option ⌥ + H

Optionally, you can use the align buttons at the top of the right hand sidebar;

Align vertical centres;

Align horizontal centres;

9. Add Fill Colour to Your Shape Layer

Now, let’s add some fill colour to our brand new layer!

Select the layer: ‘My First Button’;

Navigate to the Fill tab on the right hand side bar;

Change D9D9D9 to Maroon: #F24738

Optionally, you could have used the document colours:

Close to close the colour palette.

Click off canvas to deactivate the active shape layer.

10. Add Stroke to Shape

Select the layer: ‘My First Button’;

Navigate over to the right hand side panel and click Stroke;

This will put a default stroke value of black #000000 with stroke thickness of 1px but we will want to change that and so;

11. Change Stroke Colour on Shape

Click the Black Thumbnail beside 000000. This will activate the colour palette;

In the colour palette, drag the colour picker node all the way to the top left hand corner.

This will change the stroke colour to White.

12. Increase Stroke Thickness on Shape

Return to the Stroke side panel and increase stroke thickness to 3px.

Click off canvas to close the colour palette.

13. Add Shadow to Shape

Navigate to the right hand panel and Effects

Click Effects. This will put a default shadow of black #000000 with an opacity of 25%. But we will want to change that and so;

Click on the Sun Icon located beside Drop shadow.

This will open Effects Settings. Click on Shadow Opacity Percentage and change the value to 8%

Click off canvas to close the effects panel.

Nice one! We’ve just added a shadow to our button! Let’s add a little radius.

14. Change Shape Radius

What we have to do to start is click on our shape layer;

Navigate to the right hand panel. Near the top, you will see the properties panel. The last button is the radius tool. This is how you can make a shape round or square.

Let’s change the value from 0 to 30.

Voilà! Well done! You now have rounded corners.

15. Copy Shape Layer

Now taking everything we have learned so far, we are going to repeat the process

  1. Copy ‘My First Button’ shape
  2. Drag new shape it into ‘My Second Frame’
  3. Change the width and height property to 200px x 200px
  4. Align new shape horizontally and vertically
  5. Change Fill colour to Beige: #F2C0A2

Well done! For this next stage, it is important that our button has the exact same name on both frames.

16. Change to Prototype Mode

Let’s navigate to the top of the right hand side panel and change Figma from Design Mode to Prototype Mode

Change to Prototype Mode

You will now be in Prototype Mode.

Double check your prototype is set to iPhone 14 as, if you recall, our canvas size is for an iPhone 14.

iPhone 14 measurements: w 390 x h 844 px

17. Create a Connection

Select the button on ‘My First Frame’

Hover over the right hand edge; You will see a plus + node.

Drag the plus node across to ‘My Second Frame’. You will know the connection is made when you see a blue arrow.

Once the connection is made, at the same time, a panel called Interaction Details will pop up;

Under Animation, Click Instant and change to Smart Animate.

18. Activate Smart Animate

Figma is super intelligent! With Smart Animate activated, Figma takes layers on different frames and transitions their properties from frame to frame, as long as both of those layers share the exact same name. Let’s see!

both ‘My First Button’ layers share the same name but different properties on each Frame

My First Frame: My First Button

Size: 150px x 150px

Colour: Maroon; #F24738

My Second Frame: My First Button

Size: 200px x 200px

Colour: Beige: #F2C0A2

Smart Animate will transition those properties from one state to another. Let’s do that now, if you haven’t already done so;

Under Animation, Click Instant

Click Instant change to Smart Animate

19. Repeat the Process

Select ‘My First Button’ on ‘My Second Frame’;

Hover over the right hand edge until you see the + plus sign;

Drag the arrow across until you have connected it to the edge of ‘My First Frame’

Again, we will want to change the animation type to Smart Animate;

Voilà! You are all done! Now it’s time to play the prototype;

20. Play Your Prototype

You can use the keyboard shortcut;

On Windows:

  • Play Prototype: Ctrl + period

On Mac:

  • Play Prototype: Option ⌥ + Cmd + Return

Or Just Push Play Button located in the top right hand side of the Figma interface;

And here we are;

Open in Figma

21. Well Done! Job Completed!

Now why not get creative! Go have some fun 🤗. Change some properties from frame to frame.

  • Change the radius on the first screen, watch how it shifts to the next!
  • Change the color, alignment, change the shadows and strokes!
  • Get comfortable with these basic principals and you will be off to a great start!
  • Always remember when animating, you must keep same name on all layers!
  • 99.99% of everything you need to know to produce great prototypes, you have just completed!
