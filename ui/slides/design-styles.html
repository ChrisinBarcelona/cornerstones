Bauhaus · 1919–1933

A German school of art and design that preached functionalism: form comes from purpose.

Geometric shapes, primary colours, strong grids and clean type. Almost every modern interface owes it something.

UI Deck · Design Styles2 / 11

Skeuomorphism · around 2007–2013

Digital things that look like real objects: lined paper, leather, wood.

It made the early iPhone feel familiar.

Today's users know digital patterns well enough not to need the disguise.

UI Deck · Design Styles3 / 11

Flat design · from 2013

Solid colours and simple shapes. Fast and clean, but at its extreme it hides what's clickable.

UI Deck · Design Styles4 / 11

Brutalism · mid-2010s

Rejects polish on purpose: raw text, system fonts, underlined links and monochrome.

Honest and memorable, but easy to make hard to use.

UI Deck · Design Styles5 / 11

Neumorphism · around 2020

Soft UI: elements look pressed out of the background with gentle shadows.

Calm and tactile, but its low contrast often fails accessibility checks.

UI Deck · Design Styles6 / 11

Glassmorphism · 2020s

Frosted glass: semi-transparent panels with background blur.

Popular in operating systems and in VR and AR. Watch text contrast over busy backgrounds.

UI Deck · Design Styles7 / 11

Neobrutalism · 2020s

Brutalism with bright colour: thick black outlines, hard shadows, bold type and playful illustration.

UI Deck · Design Styles8 / 11

I hate everything that is driven by fashion.

Dieter Rams. Use styles as tools, not goals.
