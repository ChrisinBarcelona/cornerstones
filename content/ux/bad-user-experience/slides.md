@title
# Bad User Experience
Every experience makes a promise. Bad UX is the gap between the promise and the reality.
---
@statement
All design is user experience design.

The only question is whether the experience is **good** or **bad**.
---
@image
## The promise

![The Willy's Chocolate Experience website: a glossy, AI-generated candy wonderland](p-1-91044935-the-willy-wonka-experiences-generative-ai-debacle-is-just-the-s.jpg)

*Glasgow, February 2024. Tickets up to £35 for giant mushrooms, chocolate fountains and dancing Oompa-Loompas.*

@split
## The reality

![Performers in front of a printed backdrop in a sparse warehouse](240429-willy-wonka-experience-los-angeles-mn-1111-c512ca.webp)

Families arrived at a **sparsely decorated warehouse**: a few plastic props, a small bouncy castle and backdrops pinned to the walls.

The event was so bad it made international news.
---
@statement
Expectations are part of the experience.

The advert, the website and the ticket all make a promise, and the experience is **judged against it**.
---
@quote
> Good design is honest.

Dieter Rams

## Waiting feels longer than it is

![A traveller asleep on a row of airport seats](houston_airport.webp)

Houston's George Bush airport had constant complaints about **baggage claim**.

The real wait wasn't long. Passengers just spent most of it **standing still** at the carousel.
---
@statement
So the airport moved the arrival gates **further away**.

More walking, less standing: complaints dropped, though the bags came no faster.
---
@split
## Show people what's happening

![A looping loading spinner](0_3IFEy-hfoIpgFjBl.gif)

The same goes for screens. A blank wait feels broken; a **visible status** feels like progress.

An informed customer is a happy customer.
---
@split
## Signs mean different things to different people

![A car's air-recirculation button, joked about online as the button that undoes the accident](signs.jpg)

You know what your icon means. Your users have only the icon.

Test your signs with real people before you print, ship or launch them.

## People use things in ways you never expected

![Pencils printed 'Too cool to do drugs' that read 'Do drugs' once sharpened](7b6a9f0a-f79e-49e4-bcf0-cbd2cdc8131c.png)

An anti-drugs pencil, until you **sharpen it**.

Nobody tested what happens after day one. Usability testing exists to find exactly this.

@split
## A button in the wrong place

![The Lincoln MKC dashboard: the Sport button sits just above Engine Start/Stop](Slide_16_9_-_7.jpg)

On the Lincoln MKC, **Engine Start/Stop** sat right below the **Sport** button.

Drivers reaching for Sport with their eyes on the road switched the engine off at speed.
---
@statement
In 2015, Ford recalled thousands of cars and **moved the button** to the top.

A layout decision became a safety problem. In UX, details are never just details.
---
@text
## Coercive design

A close button made tiny. A "No thanks" written to make you feel foolish. A cancel link hidden three menus deep.

Patterns that fight the user may win a click. They **cost trust**, and many are now called out as *deceptive patterns*.
---
@cards
## Five ways experiences fail

- **Broken promises** Marketing sets expectations the product can't meet
- **Idle waiting** Time with nothing to do or see feels longer
- **Unclear signs** Icons and labels people read differently
- **Hidden status** People can't tell what the system is doing
- **Coercion** The design works against the user to serve the business

@exercise
## Take this keypad apart

![A cash machine keypad: a red Enter key with a cross, a green Cancel key with a circle and four blank keys](002df126-35fc-4b34-97cc-54ab99831dc1.png)

Find **three** things that could make someone press the wrong key.

Which failure does each one show? Then sketch a fix.
---
@answers
## Check yourself

- **Enter is red with a ✕, Cancel is green with a ○.** Almost every keypad uses green for Enter and red for Cancel, so this flips what people already know: *unclear signs*
- **Four blank keys.** They invite presses that do nothing, with no feedback: *hidden status*
- **Enter sits at the top**, where most keypads put Cancel, so muscle memory sends you the wrong way
- **Your redesign:** green Enter at the bottom right, red Cancel above it, yellow Clear in between, and no blank keys

Then go hunting: find three bad experiences this week and bring the worst one to class.
