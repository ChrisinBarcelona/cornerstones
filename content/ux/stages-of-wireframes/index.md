# Stages of Wireframes

### Iterative Design

Initial Hypothesis → User Stories → MVP → IA: Task Flows → **Paper Wireframes (Lo-Fi)** → **Mid-Fi Wireframes** → **Style Guide** → **Hi-Fi Wireframes** → Prototype → Test → Feedback

Here we will discuss the stages of the iterative process. How many steps you take depends on the time you have to complete the project. The important thing to remember is that the word **fidelity** simply means faithfulness to the final design: the more faithful you are to the final look, the higher the fidelity.

1. Task Flow
2. Sketching
3. Paper Wireframes (low fidelity)
4. Mid-Fi Wireframes (greyscale, in Figma)
5. Style Guide
6. Hi-Fi Wireframes
7. Prototype

---

## Task Flow

![1_wjw5RzL4JwVXjy6jaZGFjQ.png](1_wjw5RzL4JwVXjy6jaZGFjQ.png)

The Task Flow is the preliminary stage of wireframing and the lowest fidelity you can work at. A task flow is an ordered sequence of actions that a user takes to complete one task. Part of information architecture, it represents the raw logic of the prototype and nearly always comes from a broader user goal. It is a vital step before you begin wireframing.

---

## Sketching

![IMG_8847 2.heic](8280646c-dc38-453c-ad23-2c83d6a92906.png)

Sketching is a quick, optional stage where you draw ideas to explore different solutions to a problem. Sketches are usually not presented to the team; they are a starter for your own thinking.

---

## Paper Wireframes (Lo-Fi)

![wireframe_sketches.jpg](4c3ce70d-6f39-4f45-9f43-482c72817f85.png)

Paper wireframes are your **low-fidelity** wireframes: simple drawings that outline the layout and flow of each screen without final content or visuals. They use basic shapes and lines to stand for elements, so they are fast to make, cheap to throw away and easy to test with users as a paper prototype. This is where you get the logic right.

---

## Mid-Fi Wireframes

![1_ekvIYkMnJ9cmHLp4wksuwA.png](1_ekvIYkMnJ9cmHLp4wksuwA.png)

**Mid-fidelity** wireframes move your paper screens into **Figma**. They use real interface elements (buttons, inputs, navigation, real copy) placed on a grid at the right size, but no colour, imagery or brand: they are drawn in **greyscale** so that everyone discusses function and layout, not form. Build them with components and Auto Layout from the start, and the jump to high fidelity becomes a matter of swapping styles rather than redrawing screens.

---

## Style Guide

![6323267b54b08f681b93147e_styleguide_01_2x.jpg](6323267b54b08f681b93147e_styleguide_01_2x.jpg)

A style guide sets out the visual standards of a brand or product: typography, colour, logos, imagery, iconography and spacing. It keeps every screen consistent. Create it before high-fidelity work, as Figma styles and variables, so you can apply colour and type to your mid-fi screens without touching their logic.

---

## Hi-Fi Wireframes

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/chriskelly-it/image/upload/v1642538795/iPhone_12_12_Pro_19_t11bdd.png)

**High-fidelity** wireframes are as close to the finished product as it gets: the style guide applied, real content and imagery in place, and every state of every component designed. Faithfulness to the final design is around 95%.

---

## Prototype

![](https://res.cloudinary.com/chriskelly-it/image/upload/v1642538556/Prototyping_npwsev.png)

The prototype is the hi-fi wireframes **plus interaction design**: the touch points, transitions and flows linked together into an interactive mockup that you can test with users.

Not every project needs every step. Consider how much has already been done, and how much time you have, before you choose where to start and where to finish.

---

## Why it matters for UX

Each step up in fidelity costs more time, and makes people more reluctant to change what they see. Working from paper to mid-fi to hi-fi means you test and fix the logic while changes are cheap, and only spend time on polish once the flow is proven.

## Try it: one screen, three fidelities (45 minutes)

1. Pick one screen from your current project, for example a sign-up or checkout screen.
2. Draw it on paper in 5 minutes: boxes, lines and labels only.
3. Rebuild it in Figma as a mid-fi greyscale wireframe on an 8-point grid, with real copy.
4. Apply your style guide (colour, type, imagery) to make it hi-fi.
5. Put the three side by side and note which decisions you made at each stage.

## Key takeaways

- Fidelity means faithfulness to the final design: paper (lo-fi), then mid-fi greyscale in Figma, then hi-fi.
- Get the logic right at low fidelity, where changes are cheap; add style only once the flow works.
- A prototype is hi-fi wireframes plus interaction, ready to test with users.
