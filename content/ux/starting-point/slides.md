---
@statement
Projects rarely begin as a neat package of requirements.

At the start, you only ever see **fragments** of the solution.
---
@text
## Why the start is foggy

What you don't know yet is exactly what makes the beginning hard: the users, the market, the subject itself.

That's normal. **Discovery** is how you clear the fog.

The question is how much of it each project needs.
---
@cards
## Three kinds of brief

- **A clear problem** The problem and the solution are both fairly obvious
- **A vague goal** The business knows what it wants, not how to get there
- **A ready-made solution** Someone has already decided the answer
---
@text
## 1. A clear problem

*"Our hotel booking system needs to work for new branches in Jakarta, Bali and Surabaya."*

The thoughtful answer, Indonesian language support, arrives without much digging.

Lucky you. It doesn't happen often.
---
@text
## 2. A vague goal

*"We need more customers."*

This is the most common brief of all. The job becomes a question of **how**: what's best, quickest, most impactful and, above all, cheapest?

You'll need real discovery to find out.
---
@text
## 3. A ready-made solution

*"We need an AI chatbot on our homepage."*

A stakeholder believes in a technology and wants it **now**.

Sometimes they're right. Sometimes you'll be working in full sight of a hypothesis that is completely wrong. Discovery is how you find out which, with evidence rather than opinion.
---
@statement
Don't run the same process every time.

Let the **kind of starting point** decide how deep your discovery goes.
---
@cards
## How much discovery?

- **Clear problem** Confirm it quickly, then design
- **Vague goal** Research users and the market before you pick a direction
- **Ready-made solution** Test the hypothesis behind it before you build it
---
@exercise
## Diagnose these briefs

1. "Our gym app needs to show class times for our new Girona branch."
2. "We want young people to love our bank."
3. "Our restaurant needs an app with augmented-reality menus."
4. "Bookings have fallen 20% since we redesigned the checkout."

For each: what kind of brief is it, and what's the **first question** you'd ask?
---
@answers
## Check yourself

1. **A clear problem.** *Which times, which classes, and is anything different at Girona?*
2. **A vague goal.** *What does "love" look like: sign-ups, recommendations, staying? Which young people?*
3. **A ready-made solution.** *What problem should the AR menu solve, and for whom?*
4. **A clear symptom, not a clear cause.** *Where exactly in the checkout do people drop out, and why?* It needs more discovery than it looks
