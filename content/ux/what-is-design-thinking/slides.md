@title
# Design Thinking
Understand people first. Then build, test and learn.
---
@statement
**Design Thinking** is a creative problem-solving framework, built on understanding people's real, lived experience of a product or service.

Your whole sprint runs on it.
---
@split
## It's based in science

![The scientific method: observation, question, hypothesis, experiment, analysis, conclusion](scientific-method.webp)

Observe. Ask a question. Form a hypothesis. Test it. Analyse the results.

Design Thinking does the same: we let **evidence** shape the outcome, so it's stronger than our own hunch.
---
@split
## Who made it famous

![The IDEO logo](image_-1-.png)

The term was popularised by **IDEO**, the design firm in Palo Alto.

It was built to tackle even some of the world's toughest problems.
---
@cards
## Design Thinking and UX

- **Design Thinking** A problem-solving method that spans disciplines, businesses and markets
- **UX design** A discipline focused on usability and enjoyment, mostly inside the interface
- **Shared goal** Both start from what people actually need
---
@section
## The five stages
From investigation to a tested product.
---
@split
## 1. Empathise

![Stage 1: Empathise](Web_1920__38.png)

Also called **discovery**. Understand people's needs, motivations and pain points.

Interviews, observation, surveys: go and meet them.
---
@split
## 2. Define

![Stage 2: Define](Web_1920__39.png)

**Synthesise** what you found and draw conclusions.

The output is a clear problem statement: the right problem to solve.
---
@split
## 3. Ideate

![Stage 3: Ideate](Web_1920__40.png)

**Let the ideas go.** Generate many solutions before you pick one.

Quantity first, quality later.
---
@split
## 4. Prototype

![Stage 4: Prototype](Web_1920__41.png)

Turn ideas into **something people can try**: paper first, then Figma.

Cheap and quick, so you can test your assumptions.
---
@split
## 5. Test

![Stage 5: Test](Web_1920__42.png)

**Watch** real people use the prototype.

How well does it meet their needs and expectations? What surprised you?
---
@image
## A loop, not a line

![Design Thinking as a non-linear process: tests reveal insights that send you back to earlier stages](9a1209679f546ddaba8970e9511ce028.webp)

*Testing teaches you something new, and you go back: to redefine the problem, spark new ideas or learn more about users.*

1. You interview six commuters about their journey to work
2. You write: "Commuters need a way to … because …"
3. You sketch eight ideas in eight minutes
4. You link five Figma screens into a clickable flow
5. You watch someone fail to find the Pay button
6. That test shows the real problem is something else entirely. Where do you go next?

4. **Prototype**
5. **Test**
