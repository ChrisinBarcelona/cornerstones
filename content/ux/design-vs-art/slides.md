# Critical Thinking over Artistry
All design requires art. Not all art is design.
---
@split
## We work for H&M

![A model in a high-street dress: "As UX designers, we work for H&M"](Web_1920__100.png)

A high-street shirt has to fit millions of different bodies, survive the wash and sell at a price people can pay.

**UX design is the high street.**
---
@split
## Not Balenciaga

![A dramatic couture gown of coloured feathers: "Not Balenciaga"](Web_1920__113.png)

A couture gown can be breathtaking, impractical and made for one person.

That's art. Your work has to make sense to people who aren't you, who are in a hurry, and who will never read your design rationale.

## Two traditions
What art history can teach a designer.
---
@split
## Classicism

![Titian, The Allegory of Prudence, c. 1550–1565](Web_1920__85.png)

Ancient Greek and Roman principles: **form, simplicity, proportion** and clear structure, with restrained emotion and an appeal to the intellect.

Like design thinking, it's preoccupied with structure and strategy.
---
@split
## Romanticism and abstraction

![Kandinsky, Black Lines, 1913](Web_1920__87.png)

**Emotion, imagination and the individual** come first.

Kandinsky's abstract work uses shape, colour and line almost independently of the visible world.
---
@statement
Both are valuable.

But a design system, a checkout or a hospital app needs the **discipline** of the first more than the freedom of the second.
---
@cards
## Critical thinking over artistry

- **Substance** over style
- **Critical thinking** over assumptions and "experience"
- **The many** over the few
- **Universal needs** over individual taste
- **Form follows function** Still beautiful, never decadent
---
@text
## Applicability, not allegory

J.R.R. Tolkien preferred **applicability**, which lives in the freedom of the reader, to **allegory**, where the author decides the meaning.

Good UX works the same way: the user, not the designer, decides what the product means to them.

@exercise
## H&M or Balenciaga?

Which approach does each brief need, and why?

1. A hospital's appointment-booking app
2. An album cover for an experimental jazz band
3. The checkout for a supermarket chain
4. A poster for a gallery's Kandinsky exhibition
5. A design system for Barcelona's city council
---
@answers
## Check yourself

1. **H&M.** Patients of every age, often stressed: clarity first
2. **Balenciaga is fine.** It's art: one band, an audience who wants to be surprised
3. **H&M.** Millions of people, in a hurry, spending money
4. **Somewhere between.** Expression draws people in, but the date, place and price must be clear
5. **H&M.** Every citizen, every service, for years: consistency and accessibility above all
