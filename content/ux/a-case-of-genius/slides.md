# A Case of Genius
Trunki, and why great products are designed for the whole journey.
---
@split
## A suitcase you can ride

![A Gruffalo-themed Trunki: a brown ride-on suitcase with horns, a face and wheels](gruffalo-trunki-ride-on-suitcase_1.jpg)

**Trunki** is carry-on luggage for young children, designed by **Rob Law**.

Colourful, lightweight and shaped like animals and vehicles, so children can sit on it and be pulled along.
---
@statement
Rob Law pitched Trunki on the BBC's **Dragons' Den**.

The Dragons turned him down: they didn't think the product was strong enough.
---
@text
## He went ahead anyway

Trunki became one of the best-known products ever to be rejected on the show.

Rejection is feedback, not a verdict. The Dragons judged the object. Rob had designed for the **journey**.
---
@cards
## What he understood

- **Airports are busy** Long queues, long corridors, lots of waiting
- **Parents need control** Children wander off in crowds
- **Hands are full** Parents can't carry a child and their luggage
- **Kids love to ride** Every child wants to sit on a suitcase
- **Kids need stimulation** A bored child is a loud child
- **Kids need luggage** And they like having their own
---
@split
## One product, every need

![Rob Law at the airport with children riding their Trunkis](Unknown-4-scaled-e1593529717557.jpg)

The child **rides**. The parent **pulls**. The luggage comes too.

One object answers the whole family's problem, in the place where it hurts most.
---
@statement
Rob followed one of Don Norman's key ideas: make the product **pleasurable** to use.

Functional and enjoyable together beats functional alone.
---
@text
## Context is everything

Before you design, ask:

- **Where** is it used? A busy airport, not a showroom
- **When?** At the most stressful moment of a holiday
- **Who** is the customer? The parent pays; the child uses it

## Take Trunki apart

![A Gruffalo-themed Trunki ride-on suitcase](gruffalo-trunki-ride-on-suitcase_1.jpg)

Horns, saddle, wheels, strap, face.

Which need does each feature answer? Is it for the **child** or the **parent**?
---
@answers
## Check yourself

- **Horns:** handles for the child to hold on while riding: *kids love to ride*
- **Saddle shape:** a seat that is also a suitcase: *hands are full*
- **Wheels:** it rolls, so a tired child can be pulled through the airport
- **Strap:** the parent tows the child and carries it when it's empty: *parents need control*
- **Face and theme:** a character the child wants to own: *kids need stimulation*
