## Who they are

Rams is a German industrial designer, arguably the most influential product designer of the last century. He joined the electronics company **Braun** in 1955 and was its head of design from 1961 to 1995, turning radios, record players, calculators, shavers and clocks into calm, honest, useful objects. His motto, *Weniger, aber besser* ("less, but better"), is still the clearest summary of good design there is.

## Key contributions

- **The ten principles for good design.** Good design is innovative, makes a product useful, is aesthetic, makes a product understandable, is unobtrusive, is honest, is long-lasting, is thorough down to the last detail, is environmentally friendly, and is as little design as possible.
- **Braun's design language.** Simple forms, a calm grey and white palette, clear controls and one accent colour where it mattered. You can see it today in Apple's products: the iPod echoes his T3 pocket radio, and the original iPhone calculator echoes his ET 66.
- **Design that lasts.** His Vitsœ 606 shelving has been in production since 1960 and is designed to be repaired and extended, not thrown away. Rams has spoken for decades against waste, novelty for its own sake and planned obsolescence.

## Why it matters for UX

The ten principles read like a checklist for interface design: useful, understandable, honest, unobtrusive, thorough and as little design as possible. When you remove a button, cut a step from a flow or refuse a dark pattern because "good design is honest", you are working in Rams's tradition. He is the reason "less, but better" is the first rule many designers learn.
