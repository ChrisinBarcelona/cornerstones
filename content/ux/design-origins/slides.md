@title
# Design Origins
User experience is as old as the first tool.
---
@statement
All design is user experience design.

The only question is whether it's a **good** experience or a **bad** one.
---
@text
## Design comes from necessity

The best design has always started with a **real problem** that needed solving, not with making things for the sake of it.

That matters more than ever, now that AI makes it easy to create endlessly.
---
@split
## The first designer

![Three ancient stone spear points, bound to wooden shafts](photo_1353010363546-1-0.jpg)

The oldest stone tools ever found are around **3.3 million years old**.

Imagine the problem: you want to hunt a deer, and bare hands won't do. You need a spear.
---
@cards
## Three requirements, then and now

- **Sharp enough** → **Functionality** What it does
- **Light enough to carry** → **Accessibility** Where it can be used
- **Strong enough** → **Consistency** How often it can be used
---
@split
## Take critique well

![A cartoon cave family telling their toolmaker the last spear wasn't sharp enough](Artboard__4.png)

Making a spear took planning, and shared knowledge.

When the first one failed, someone said so. Learn to take design critique well.
---
@cards
## Experience through the ages

- **Ancient Greece** Theatres designed so every seat could hear the actors
- **Byzantium** Mosaics creating a whole visual world: a design system of its own
- **Nuremberg, c. 1510** Peter Henlein, often credited with the first pocket watch
- **1829** William Burt patents the "typographer", an early typewriter
---
@section
## The birth of the internet
When the computer became for everyone.
---
@text
## From research network to the web

**1969:** ARPANET, a US research network, sends its first messages.

**1 January 1983:** ARPANET switches to TCP/IP, the protocol behind the global internet.

Early users were engineers and technicians, so nobody thought much about the user. The **personal computer** changed that: now computers were for everyone.
---
@split
## The early web

![Netscape's home page in the mid-1990s: grey toolbar, a banner and blue links](Screen-Shot-2015-08-03-at-10.webp)

Basic styling, blue links, pages loading over a dial-up modem.

As the web grew in the mid-1990s, the conversation about **user experience** began.
---
@statement
In **1993**, Don Norman joined Apple as **User Experience Architect**.

The first time "user experience" appeared in a job title.
---
@split
## The web goes to market

![The AuctionWeb logo over eBay's](3-ebay.webp)

In 1995, Pierre Omidyar spent a long weekend coding **AuctionWeb**, to bring buyers and sellers together.

It became **eBay**.
---
@text
## Design thinking

In 1991, **IDEO** was founded in Palo Alto, bringing together David Kelley's studio and others.

With pioneers such as **Tim Brown**, it popularised a human-centred, staged method for solving problems: **Design Thinking**.

From the mid-1990s on, UX became a major part of how technology is made.
