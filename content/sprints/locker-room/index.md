# Sprint 02: Locker Room

![A pink and turquoise locker hut under neon Locker Room signage on a beach at sunset, palm trees behind it](locker-room.jpg)

> Validating a gap in the tourist industry.

## The brief

In Barceloneta, during the peak summer season, the beach has two problems. **One: crime happens.** Bags, phones and wallets left on the sand while people swim are an easy target. **Two: people have nowhere to keep their things safely.** So they take turns to swim, bury their phones in the sand, or simply stay out of the water.

**Locker Room** is a startup concept: secure, colourful lockers right on the beach, which you find, book, pay for and open with an app. Your job is to find out whether people really want it, who they are, how it could work as a business, and to design the app.

This is a **Lean Startup** sprint. You are not just designing an app: you are testing whether the business should exist at all.

## The problem

Beachgoers, tourists and locals alike, have no safe, convenient way to leave their belongings while they swim. Worry spoils the day, and theft can ruin a holiday. But a good idea is not the same as a good business: we don't yet know who would pay, how much, or how often.

## Your challenge

**How might we** let people at Barceloneta enjoy the sea without worrying about their belongings, in a way that works as a business?

## Sprint goals

- **Understand current behaviour.** What do people do with their things today when they swim?
- **Find evidence for desirability.** Do people actually want this, and would they pay?
- **Understand the customer base.** Who are the customers: tourists, locals, families, groups, sports people?
- **Understand user needs.** What does "safe" and "convenient" mean to them?
- **Understand the business model.** How does Locker Room make money, and what does it cost to run?
- **Design the app.** Find a locker, book, pay, open and close it, extend time, and get help.

## Scope

**In scope**
- Barceloneta beach in peak season.
- Research with real beachgoers.
- The business model and the riskiest assumptions behind it.
- The mobile app: from finding a locker to collecting your things.

**Out of scope**
- Designing the physical locker hut (the image is the concept).
- Building a working product, or licences and permits.
- Other beaches or cities, unless your research points there.

## Deliverables

1. **Lean UX Canvas** and a **long-term goal**.
2. **How Might We** questions, **research goals** and **research questions**.
3. **Business Model Canvas** with the riskiest assumptions marked.
4. **User survey** results and **user interviews** with real beachgoers.
5. **Personas** built from your research.
6. **Paper, mid-fi and hi-fi prototypes** of the app, tested with users.
7. **Mood board, brand pack** and an **A/B test** of a key screen.
8. **DVF analysis** of your features, and a general-audience presentation.

## Constraints and tips

- **Talk to real people on the beach.** Your classmates are not your customers.
- **Separate what people say from what they do.** Watch what they do with their bags before you ask.
- **Be ready to kill the idea.** If the evidence says nobody would pay, that is a valid and valuable result. Show it.
- **Speak business.** Back your design decisions with the Business Model Canvas and your DVF scores.

## Success looks like

A clear, evidence-backed answer to "should Locker Room exist?", a business model that holds up to questions, and a tested app that makes finding, booking and opening a locker feel effortless.
