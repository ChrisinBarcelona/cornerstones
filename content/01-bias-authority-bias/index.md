# 01. Bias: Authority Bias

Authority bias, also known as the authority effect or deference to authority, refers to a cognitive bias in which individuals attribute greater accuracy, credibility, or validity to the statements or actions of an authority figure. This bias arises from the tendency of humans to rely on external sources of expertise and knowledge, particularly when they perceive someone as being in a position of power, expertise, or social status.

---

Authority bias can have both positive and negative implications. In some cases, deferring to experts or authoritative figures can be beneficial, as they often possess knowledge and experience that can guide decision-making and problem-solving. However, the bias becomes problematic when it leads individuals to unquestioningly accept and follow authority, even in situations where the authority figure may be mistaken or promoting misinformation.

---

This bias can contribute to instances of groupthink, where individuals conform to the beliefs or decisions of a perceived authority figure or dominant group, suppressing their own critical thinking and independent judgment. 

Being aware of authority bias is important for developing critical thinking skills and making well-informed decisions. It means finding credible research. It involves actively questioning and evaluating the source information and opinions, seeking additional perspectives, counter balance arguments and considering evidence-based reasoning rather than solely relying on the authority!
