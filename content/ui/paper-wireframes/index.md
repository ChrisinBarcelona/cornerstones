# Paper Wireframes Tips

Paper Wireframes allows you to slow down and really think through the UI before jumping into Figma. 

![wireframe_sketches.jpg](wireframe_sketches.jpg)

## Find your own method

Each designer should develop their own independent workflow and gain confidence in that method over time. Where some designers generally prefer to use text, others will prefer shape. Ultimately the most important thing is that the concept art is interpretable by a wider team and what you are trying to represent speaks clearly within the UI.  

![photo-1600132806608-231446b2e7af-1.jpg](photo-1600132806608-231446b2e7af-1.jpg)

## Pencil first, then pen

Once you have your finished wireframes in pencil, it adds intensity to go over the lines with a sharpie pen.

![](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1559743340-e9baff3f1052?ixlib=rb-4.0.3&q=85&fm=jpg&crop=entropy&cs=srgb)

## Drawing common elements

In the example below we have a card presented in 3 different ways. Each card has a photo, a title and a description. 

In the first example, I have used a square with an X through it to represent a picture, the title is represented with a half line and the description is represented with two lines and a half line. The second example reflects the same thing with the difference being I have added text for the title. The third example has the picture represented with two triangles and a line.

## Icons

When it comes to iconography, use universally recognisable, unambiguous iconography. By this I suggest using only those icons that require practically no interpretation and avoid icons which are difficult to interpret. 

![Frame 10.jpg](7295ad21-e86e-4172-ac45-f82140325b25.png)

Whilst we can go as far as to run an icon usability test, generally, we should avoid icons that could mislead the user. Instead, when drawing, simply use circles to represent icons. Given the fact icon placements frequently change it’s always good to limit your icons: 

1. home 

2. hamburger 

3. close 

4. like 

5. forward arrow 

6. back arrow

![Frame 9.jpg](fc6d090a-3eb0-4c51-81b3-5447e91dc58d.png)

Ultimately, no matter what, always aim to keep your work consistent, on the grid, interpretable and minimalist with as little cognitive load as possible.

## Why it matters for UX

Paper is the fastest, cheapest place to make mistakes. A few minutes with pencil and pen lets you test layouts and flows before you spend hours in Figma, and a shared drawing language means your whole team can read your wireframes.

## Try it: wireframe one flow on paper (30 minutes)

1. Pick a three-screen flow from your project, for example browse → detail → checkout.
2. Draw each screen in pencil inside a phone outline, using the conventions on this page.
3. Go over the final lines in pen.
4. Show the screens to a classmate and ask them to tell you what each element is. Fix anything they misread.

## Key takeaways

- Paper wireframes let you think through the UI before you open Figma.
- Use a consistent drawing language that your whole team can read.
- Keep icons simple and few; use circles when in doubt.
