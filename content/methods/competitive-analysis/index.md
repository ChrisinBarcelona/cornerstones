# Competitive Analysis

# 🪜 Stage: Define

Analyse competitors to identify opportunities and inform design direction.

# 👋 Context

Now we should have a robust understanding of the problem space and a clear direction about tour next steps, take a look at how others are currently solving this problem through competitive analysis. 

A competitive analysis is used to examine how your current problem is being solved by competitors both [directly and indirectly](https://www.google.com/search?q=direct+vs+indirect+competitors&oq=direct+vs+indirect+compe&gs_lcrp=EgZjaHJvbWUqBwgAEAAYgAQyBwgAEAAYgAQyBwgBEAAYgAQyBwgCEAAYgAQyBggDEEUYOTIHCAQQABiABDIHCAUQABiABDIICAYQABgWGB4yCAgHEAAYFhgeMggICBAAGBYYHjIICAkQABgWGB7SAQg1MTM1ajBqOagCBrACAfEFRXOQTv_8oL4&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8). This analysis helps you to identify market opportunities, set benchmarks for performance, and develop strategies to gain a competitive edge.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

By the end of this activity, you will be able to **systematically analyse competitors**, identify opportunities for differentiation, and generate actionable insights to guide your design decisions.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

1. Identify 3–5 competitors relevant to your product or service, including both direct and indirect competitors.
2. Investigate each competitor focusing on:
    - **Features and offerings**
    - **User experience and usability**
    - **Branding, positioning, and pricing**
    - **Strengths and weaknesses**
    - **Successfulness**
    - **Core Feature**
    - **Others Features**
3. Reflect on your findings by answering:
    - What are common patterns or trends across competitors?
    - Which gaps or unmet needs exist that your solution could address?
    - **Are there strategies or features from competitors that could inspire your design**?
4. Summarise your competitive analysis by documenting:
    - Key insights and takeaways
    - Opportunities for differentiation
    - How this analysis informs your design direction

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- 30–40 minutes

# 👉 Extra Notes

- Include both direct and indirect competitors; inspiration can come from unexpected sources.
- Focus on **meaningful insights**, not exhaustive details.
- Visual aids such as screenshots, sketches, or diagrams can help clarify your observations.

# ⚡ Tips

- Think strategically: you’re looking for insights that inform your solution.
- Link your findings to user research from the discovery phase.
- Keep your summary concise and focused on actionable takeaways.
