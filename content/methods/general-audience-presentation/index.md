# General Audience Presentation

> UX Strategy is only as good as your ability to communicate it.
> 

# 🪜 Stage: Retrospective

Five minutes to tell the story of your sprint, in your own voice, thrills and spills included.

# 👋 Context

A **general audience presentation** comes at the end of a sprint. It covers the same ground as a [Checkpoint Presentation](checkpoint-presentation.html), but it tells it as a **personal journey**. Your audience wasn't in the room for your interviews and site visits, so take them with you: your own voice should be there from the first sentence to the last.

Don't make it all "hunky dory". Nobody believes a sprint where everything went to plan, and nobody remembers one either. Show the **thrills and spills**: the interview that changed your mind, the idea that flopped and how you recovered. That's what makes the story yours, and what makes it credible.

Keep the sense of **curiosity**, and show how you used UX methods to **plot your research direction**: which method you chose, what it told you and where it sent you next. Keep it **under five minutes**.

These are two separate types of presentation:

- A **checkpoint** is a working update for people who know the brief. It's built to be questioned.
- A **general audience presentation** is a story for people who don't. It's built to be understood and remembered.

## Three things to cover

Whatever story you tell, keep it focused on three things:

1. **What you wanted to know.**
2. **What you gained an understanding of.**
3. **Which ideas and concepts remain to be tested and taken forward.**

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- Tell the story of your sprint as a personal journey, in your own voice, in under five minutes.
- Show how UX methods set your research direction, without turning the talk into a list of methods.
- Back your insights with user quotes, relevant points or major data points.
- Be honest about what went wrong, and end with what's still to test and take forward.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

1. **Open with your curiosity 🔍.** What did you want to know, and why did it grab you? Start with the question, not the brief.
2. **Plot your route 🗺️.** Show how your methods set your research direction: "The survey surprised us, so we went back for interviews…". Use methods as signposts on the journey, not as a checklist.
3. **Share what you understood 💡.** Pick your strongest insights and give each one its evidence: a user quote, a relevant point or a major data point.
4. **Show the thrills and spills 🎢.** The moment it clicked, the moment it fell apart, and what you did next. One high and one low is enough.
5. **Use your own voice 🎙️.** Say "I" for what you did and "we" for the team. Tell it the way you'd tell a friend, and don't read your slides.
6. **End with what's next 🧭.** Which ideas and concepts remain to be tested and taken forward?
7. **Rehearse and time it ⏱️.** Run it out loud, to a classmate if you can. If it's over five minutes, cut detail, not the story.

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- 5 minutes, plus questions

# 👉 Extra Notes

**Presenting UX work: five simple best practices**

- **Lead with the insight.** Put the most important finding first and the detail after, like the [inverted pyramid](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/inverted-pyramid/) Nielsen Norman Group recommends for writing. If the audience only remembers one thing, make sure it's the insight.
- **Tell a story.** Raw data rarely convinces anyone on its own. A narrative connects your data to why people should care ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persuasive-storytelling/)).
- **Know your audience.** A general audience doesn't know your jargon. Use their language and answer what they care about ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persuasive-storytelling/)).
- **Show the evidence.** Ground every claim in research: direct user quotes, observed behaviour and the numbers that matter. Pair a statistic with a short anecdote so people can picture it.
- **End with next steps.** Finish on what you'll test and take forward, so the audience leaves knowing where the story goes next.

# 🏞️ Template

- 🔍 **What I wanted to know:** the question, and why I was curious
- 🗺️ **My route:** the methods that set my research direction
- 💡 **What I understood:** the key insights, each with a quote, point or data point
- 🎢 **Thrills and spills:** a high, a low and how I recovered
- 🧭 **What's next:** the ideas and concepts still to test and take forward
- ⏱️ Under five minutes

# ⚡ Tips

- Start with a person, not a process: a user, a quote or a moment from your research.
- Show one artifact per step of the journey, only where you learned something.
- Explain any UX term you use, or don't use it.
- The low point is often the most memorable part. Don't rush it.
- Practise the first and last 30 seconds until you can say them without notes.
