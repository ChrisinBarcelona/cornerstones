# Usability Test

# 🪜 Stage: Validation
# 👋 Context

Now that you have completed your prototype, now it’s time to test it with users to get feedback. 

This is where [usability testing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-testing-101/) comes into play. Unlike interviews, usability tests are structured sessions where participants complete a task while the facilitator observes. Your role is not to teach or lead them to the “right” path, but to uncover where the product helps or hinders. Importantly you are there to facilitate. You shouldn’t really be talking. You just set the objective and observe. 

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

By the end of this activity, students will have facilitated five user interviews and identified usability issues that can be brought into a design iteration. 

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

### **Step 1 – Watch Videos and Prepare a Usability Test Plan**

**Instructions:**

1. **Revisit your Research Goal:**
    - Clarify the purpose of the test (e.g., testing a new onboarding flow, evaluating navigation, checking if users can complete a task without help).
    - Ensure the goal aligns with project objectives.
2. **Select the Test Type:**
    - Choose between an online or in person style of testing.
3. **Understand the Task:**
    - Identify the task that users should attempt. Chances are that you will be asking them to fulfil the task set out from your task flow. Never give two tasks at once.
4. **Craft Neutral Instructions:**
    - Frame tasks without revealing the solution.
    - Example: Instead of “Click the Help button,” say “Show me how you would get help if you were stuck.”

![H2x1_NSwitchDS_Cluedo (1).jpg](H2x1_NSwitchDS_Cluedo_-1-.jpg)

*Good task instructions are like clues in a board game. it’s no fun (and not useful) if you’re told where to go and what to do.*

1. **Plan for Probing (Moderated):**
    - Have gentle prompts ready, but avoid leading.
    - Examples: “What are you thinking right now?” “What made you decide to click there?”
2. **Anticipate Challenges:**
    - Participants may go silent, look for approval, or ask for help.
    - Strategy: Follow the facilitator in the video — stay neutral, remind them there are no right or wrong answers, and encourage them to think aloud.

---

### **Step 2 – Conduct the Usability Test**

**Instructions:**

1. **Recruit Participants:**
    - Match participants to the intended users of the product.
    - Use screening questions without revealing too much about what’s being tested.
2. **Set Up the Environment:**
    - Quiet room, screen recording tools, consent forms ready.
    - If online testing, setup [Notta.ai](http://Notta.ai) or [tl;dv.io](https://tldv.io/) to work within Google Meetup
    - Ensure tech works (camera, microphone, software).
3. **Build Rapport:**
    - Like in the video: start with small talk, explain that the product is being tested — not the participant.
    - Reassure: “We’re looking at how the product works for you, not how well you perform.”
4. **Facilitate the Test:**
    - Tell the participant what you would like them to achieve.
    - Encourage “think aloud” — ask participants to narrate their thought process.
    - Stay neutral (avoid hints like “That’s correct”).
    - DO NOT tell them where to go or provide instructions.
5. **Record Observations:**
    - Note where participants hesitate, fail, succeed, or express frustration.
    - Capture time on task, errors, and emotional cues.
6. **Conclude the Test:**
    - Ask debrief questions: “What worked well for you?” “What felt frustrating?”

**Outcome: Students will have collected qualitative data that can be used to identity trends and pain points.**

---

### **Step 3 – Look for Patterns**

**Instructions:**

1. **After Interviews, Review Recordings and Notes:**
    - Watch or listen back to sessions to catch details you might have missed in real time.
    - Pay attention to moments of hesitation, confusion, or delight.
2. **Identify Common Issues:**
    - Look across multiple participants for recurring struggles, workarounds, or misunderstandings.
    - Example: If three participants can’t find the checkout button, that’s a pattern worth investigating.
3. **Cluster Themes:**
    - Group similar observations into categories such as “Navigation issues,” “Content clarity,” “Button visibility.”
    - Tools like sticky notes, Miro, or Dovetail can help visualise these clusters.
4. **Leverage AI Tools:**
    - Use [Notta.ai](http://notta.ai/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) or other transcription tools to quickly synthesise interviews into searchable transcripts.
    - Highlight recurring phrases, pain points, and emotional cues.

**Outcome:** A clear set of prioritised usability themes, backed by multiple data points, ready for the next step, the design iteration.

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- **Preparation (plan)**: 30–40 min
- **Each test**: 30–45 min
- **Analysis & synthesis**: 60–90 min

# ⚡ Tips

- Test with five people per round; you'll find most of the major problems.
- Recruit people who match your persona, not classmates.
- Run a pilot session first to catch broken links and unclear tasks.
- Let participants struggle: silence and patience reveal the real problems.
- Debrief straight after each session while it's fresh.

# 📝 Summary

**Usability Tests** are the gold standard for uncovering design flaws that prevent users from completing key tasks. They differ from user interviews in that they focus on observed behaviour with a real product or prototype, not just reported feelings. By preparing clear, neutral tasks, observing carefully, and facilitating with empathy, like in the video, you’ll gather actionable insights that directly improve usability and user satisfaction ready for going forward.
