# Medium Fidelity (Mid-Fi)

# 🪜 Stage: Ideation

From paper wireframes to medium-fidelity wireframes.

# 👋 Context

Now that you’ve created your paper wireframes, the next step is to bring them into the digital space. This exercise is about taking your sketches and building them out as **medium-fidelity (mid-fi) wireframes**, in **Figma** using the Mid-Fi kit, or in **Miro**.

Nielsen Norman Group describes fidelity on three dimensions: **visuals**, **content** and **interactivity**. Paper wireframes are low on all three. Hi-fi prototypes are high on all three. Mid-fi sits in between: real layout, spacing and hierarchy, realistic labels and headings, simple clickable links, but **no colour, no brand and no final imagery**. NN/g's "Goldilocks principle" applies: just enough fidelity to answer the questions you are testing, and no more.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

By the end of this activity, students will have:

- Translated their paper sketches into **mid-fidelity wireframes** in Figma or Miro.
- Focused only on the **essential screens** required for the chosen task flow.
- Practiced reducing ideas to the **MVP level of detail**.
- Understood that mid-fi wireframes are a tool for **research and iteration**, not final design.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

1. **Open your tool.** Either duplicate the [Mid-Fi kit in Figma](https://www.figma.com/design/q8ESY2a6UxSNgTQZFJ3cJh/Figma-Exercises?node-id=2-4&p=f&t=RyxI676le4DQIyl4-0) into your drafts, or open a new **Miro** board and use its wireframe library.
2. Recreate your paper wireframes using the kit's **ready-made blocks**: boxes, text, image placeholders, buttons and navigation.
3. Build only the **screens needed** for your task flow.
4. Keep everything **monochrome and minimal**, but get the **layout, spacing and hierarchy** right.
5. Replace "lorem ipsum" with **real headings, labels and button text**.
6. Link the screens with simple clicks so the task flow can be walked through in a usability test.

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- ~2 hours

# 👉 Extra Notes

- Don’t add colours, brand or final images.
- Focus on **layout, hierarchy, and clarity**.
- Don’t include complex interaction points: simple taps and links only.

# ⚡ Tips

- You are **not designing the full application**.
- Focus only on the **task flow** from the previous exercise.
- Mid-fidelity wireframes are a **research method**: the goal is to build something that can be reviewed, tested, critiqued, and improved.
- Think of this as creating an **MVP (Minimum Viable Product)**: the simplest version that communicates your idea clearly.
- Keep everything **simple and greyscale**, so feedback is about structure, not style.
- Use **real words** wherever you can; use consistent placeholders (e.g. “Image”) only for images.

# 📝 Summary

Medium-fidelity wireframes bring your sketches into a digital format so they can be tested and discussed. By keeping them greyscale but getting the layout, hierarchy and real labels right, and focusing on the **core task flow**, students avoid distractions and stay focused on what matters most: learning whether their structure supports the user’s needs. This stage reinforces the principle of building **only the MVP** and treating wireframes as part of the **research and iteration process**, not as final deliverables.
