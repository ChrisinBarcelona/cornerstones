# User Interview

# 🪜 **Stage: User Interviews**

## 👋 Context

You’ve already launched a **survey** but sometimes you need to **go deeper** into someone’s story, to uncover the “why” behind their answers and observe how they think, feel, and act.

This is where [user interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-interviews/) come in to play. Unlike surveys, user interviews are informal conversations that allow you to understand user’s real experience with a product or service. Your role is not to interrogate, control a narrative or push for solutions, but simply gain empathy and **facilitate a conversation** that reveals lived experiences in rich detail.

User interviews occur early in the process and are **generative in nature**. It’s important that you can distinguish the difference between a user interview and a usability test, which involves a final prototyped solution. See below. 

| Aspect | User Interview | Usability Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | To generate new knowledge about your users, their experiences, needs, and pain points | To assess a design: how it performs, what’s not working, and why |
| **Research type** | Attitudinal: we collect participants’ reported behaviors, thoughts, and feelings | Behavioral: we directly observe how users interact with a design |
| **When used** | Empathize stage of the design-thinking model (or in discovery) | Test stage of the design-thinking model (or when a team is working on a product in alpha or beta) |
| **Design** | Absent: participants don’t review or try a design | Present: participants interact with a design |
| **Facilitator–participant interaction** | More natural interaction: regular eye contact, facilitators are warmer and approachable | More rigid interaction: sporadic eye contact; facilitators avoid being too friendly |

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

By the end of this activity, you will have identified real world reoccurring themes or tendencies that users have had in the context of your problem space. 

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

### **Step 1 – Prepare an Interview Guide**

**Instructions:**

1. **Define the Research Goal:**
    - Clarify the purpose of the interview (e.g., understanding user needs, identifying pain points).
    - Ensure the goal aligns with the overall project objectives.
2. **Select the Interview Type:**
    - Choose between unstructured, semi-structured, or structured interviews based on the research goal.
    - For exploratory research, a semi-structured approach is often most effective.([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/stakeholder-interviews/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
3. **Develop Key Topics:**
    - Identify 3–5 main topics to cover, ensuring they are open-ended and relevant to the research goal.
    - Example topics: "User's experience with onboarding," "Challenges faced during task completion," "Expectations from the product."
4. **Craft Interview Questions:**
    - Begin with broad, open-ended questions to encourage storytelling.
    - Use the funnel technique: start with general questions and gradually move to more specific ones.
    - Include follow-up questions to probe deeper into responses.([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/the-funnel-technique-in-qualitative-user-research/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
5. **Plan for Probing:**
    - Prepare a list of probing questions to explore responses further.
    - Examples: "Can you elaborate on that?" "Why do you think that is?"([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/the-funnel-technique-in-qualitative-user-research/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
6. **Anticipate Challenges:**
    - Consider potential challenges such as participant bias or leading questions.
    - Plan strategies to mitigate these issues, like neutral phrasing and active listening.
    

---

### **Step 2 – Conduct Your Interviews**

**Instructions:**

1. **Recruit Participants:**
    - Select participants who match the target user profile.
    - Use screening questions to ensure appropriate selection without revealing the study's purpose.([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/screening-questions-select-research-participants/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))
    - 🚨 It’s possible you will conduct street interview, pending on course needs.
2. **Set Up the Environment:**
    - Choose a quiet, comfortable setting for the interview.
    - Ensure all necessary equipment (e.g., recording devices, consent forms) is prepared in advance.
    - 🚨 Again, It’s possible you will conduct street interview, pending on course needs.
3. **Build Rapport:**
    - Start with casual conversation to make the participant comfortable.
    - Explain the purpose of the interview and ensure confidentiality.
4. **Conduct the Interview:**
    - Follow the interview guide, but remain flexible to explore interesting avenues that arise.
    - Use probing questions to delve deeper into responses.
    - Avoid leading questions and be mindful of non-verbal cues.
5. **Record Observations:**
    - Take detailed notes, focusing on key insights and non-verbal behaviors.
    - If recording, ensure the participant's consent is obtained.
    - 🚨 It’s possible you will conduct street interview, pending on course needs. If so, take photos for case study  📸
6. **Conclude the Interview:**
    - Thank the participant for their time and insights.
    - Ask if they have any final thoughts or questions.
    - Provide information on how the findings will be used.([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/the-funnel-technique-in-qualitative-user-research/?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Outcome:** A set of rich, qualitative data ready for analysis.

# ⏱️ Duration

- **Preparation (guide)**: 30–40 min
- **Each interview**: 20–30 min
- **Insight gathering:** 60 mins

# ✅ Checklist

### **Preparation**

- [ ]  Review your research to date.
- [ ]  Complete your [**Interview Guide**](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interview-guide/) with open-ended and follow-up questions.
- [ ]  Recruit **5 participants** matching your target users.

### **Before the Interview**

- [ ]  Schedule interviews at convenient times.
- [ ]  Prepare a **quiet setting** and check recording tools.
- [ ]  Have **consent forms** ready (If necessary).

### **During the Interview**

- [ ]  Build **rapport** with participants.
- [ ]  Follow your **guide** but stay flexible.
- [ ]  Use **probing questions** and note non-verbal cues.
- [ ]  Avoid **leading questions**.

### **After the Interview**

- [ ]  Thank participants and explain next steps.
- [ ]  Review recordings/notes to **identify patterns and insights**.
- [ ]  Summarise findings for design or research discussion.
- [ ]  Optionally, use tools like [**Notta.ai**](http://notta.ai/) for transcription and analysis.

# 📝 Summary

**User Interview** are by far the best way to develop insight quickly to understand the **real lived experience** of users. They provide a structured way to uncover needs, frustrations, and behaviours. By preparing a guide, in advance and screening your users, you will conduct interviews like a pro and gather insights that can inform some really useful design decisions and identify opportunities for product improvement.
