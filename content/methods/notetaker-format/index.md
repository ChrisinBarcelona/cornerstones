# Notetaker Format

# 🪜 Stage: Validation

Be invisible, and capture what people say, do, think and feel.

# 👋 Context

In every test session, the notetaker has one job: to take notes. Sometimes you can record the session, but recording can make participants feel under pressure, so often the notetaker is the record. You stay silent: a little eye contact and a friendly smile, but essentially you are invisible.

Good notes capture two kinds of signal:

- **Explicit signals:** what the participant **says** (useful quotes) and **does** (where they click, where they struggle, where they move quickly).
- **Implicit signals:** what they **think** and **feel**, shown through non-verbal cues: pauses, stutters, sighs, laughs, frowns, confusion or relief.

People often say one thing while their face says something completely different. Catching that difference is the notetaker's superpower.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- Capture key facts about each participant during the context questions.
- Record explicit signals (quotes and actions) and implicit signals (non-verbal cues).
- Keep notes in a structure that is easy to compare across sessions.
- Turn the notes into findings straight after the session.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

1. **Prepare the sheet (5 min).** One notes sheet per participant, using the template below.
2. **During the context questions,** note the participant's key data: name (or participant number), occupation, interests, and how often they face the problem.
3. **During the tasks,** for each task note: did they complete it? Where did they hesitate or struggle? Exact quotes in quotation marks. Non-verbal cues in brackets, e.g. *[long pause]*, *[laughs]*, *[frowns at the map]*.
4. **Use timestamps** if the session is recorded, so you can find key moments later.
5. **Stay silent.** If you have a question, write it down and hand it to the facilitator for the debrief.
6. **Debrief together (10 min, straight after).** With the facilitator, mark the top three observations and fill in the empathy map: says, does, thinks, feels.

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- ~30 min per session, plus a 10-minute debrief

# ⚡ Tips

- Write what you observe, not what you conclude: "clicked Back three times", not "was confused".
- Exact words are gold. Put them in quotation marks.
- Use the same sheet for every participant so patterns jump out.
- Never react to what the participant does: no nodding, gasping or laughing at mistakes.
- Anonymise as you go: use P1, P2 rather than names.

# ✨ AI Assist

After the debrief, give Claude your anonymised notes from all sessions and ask: "Which observations appear for three or more participants? Quote the evidence." Check every pattern against your own notes before you trust it (see Data Synthesis with AI).

# 📝 Summary

The **notetaker** is the session's memory. Silent and invisible, they record what participants say and do, and what they seem to think and feel, in one consistent format, so patterns across sessions are easy to spot.
