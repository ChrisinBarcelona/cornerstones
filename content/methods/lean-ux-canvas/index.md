# Lean UX Canvas

# 🪜 Stage: Discovery

First framing of the problem.

# 👋 Context

The **Lean UX Canvas** is a good place to start: it helps you generate ideas and aligns a wider audience on the problem you are solving, who it’s for and why it matters. It’s lightweight, collaborative and focused on quick learning and idea generation.

The canvas, created by Jeff Gothelf, fits on one page and has **eight boxes**. You fill them in roughly in number order, starting with the business problem and ending with the smallest experiment that will teach you something.

Early in the sprint you only have assumptions about the problem and the people, so **this exercise covers boxes 1–4**. Boxes 5–8 (solutions, hypotheses, what to learn first and the smallest experiment) need ideas and evidence you don’t have yet. You’ll come back to them later in the sprint.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- **Complete boxes 1–4 of a Lean UX Canvas** to clarify the problem, the users and the value for users.
- Know what boxes 5–8 are for, so you can pick the canvas up again later in the sprint.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

1. **Review the canvas.** Read all eight boxes in the diagram above as a team, so everyone knows where the work is heading.
2. **Fill in boxes 1–4 only, in number order:**
    - **1. Business Problem:** what issue are we solving?
    - **2. Business Outcomes:** how will we measure success?
    - **3. Users:** who experiences this problem, and who should we focus on first?
    - **4. User Outcomes & Benefits:** what value will users gain if we succeed?
3. **Write down your current assumptions.** They don’t have to be “right”; mark the ones you’ll need to check with research.
4. **Leave boxes 5–8 empty.** Don’t jump to solutions yet. You’ll return to them once you have research to build on.

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- 25–30 minutes

# 👉 Extra Notes

- Capture your **current assumptions**: they don’t have to be “right.”
- Keep it **concise and testable.**
- You’ll revisit and revise boxes 1–4 as you gather evidence, and fill in boxes 5–8 later in the sprint.

# ⚡ Tips

- Avoid vague answers (e.g. “make users happy”). Be specific.
- Think in terms of **measurable outcomes**, not just outputs.
- Use language your **team and stakeholders** will easily understand.
- If you catch yourself describing a feature, park it: it belongs in box 5, later.

# ✅ Checklist

☐ Business Problem is **clear and specific**

☐ Business Outcomes are **measurable**

☐ Users are **defined, not generic**

☐ User Outcomes describe **real value**

☐ Boxes 5–8 are **left for later in the sprint**

# 📝 Summary

The **Lean UX Canvas** provides a structured way to articulate problems, success measures and value for users. By completing boxes 1–4, students clarify the foundation of their research and design work, ready to come back to boxes 5–8 later in the sprint.
