# Business Model Canvas

# 🪜 Stage: Define

How does the idea make, deliver and capture value? All on one page.

# 👋 Context

A great experience that nobody pays for doesn't survive. In Business Week you move from "what do users need?" to "how would this work as a business?".

The **Business Model Canvas**, created by Alexander Osterwalder and Strategyzer, describes any business in **nine building blocks** on a single page. The right side is about the market (who you serve and how); the left side is about what it takes to serve them (activities, resources, partners and costs). The **value proposition** sits in the middle, connecting the two.

For a UX designer it is a reality check. Nielsen Norman Group reminds us that product **viability** is partly a design responsibility, because it depends on what users actually do. The canvas makes your assumptions about the business visible, so the team can question them, and test them, just like design assumptions.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- Describe your product idea in the nine blocks of the Business Model Canvas.
- Connect your user research (segments, needs) to how the product creates and captures value.
- Spot the riskiest business assumptions so they can be tested.
- Speak the language of product and business stakeholders.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

Use the canvas template in **FigJam** or **Miro** and fill it with sticky notes, in this order:

1. **Customer Segments:** Who are we creating value for? Who are the most important? (Use your personas.)
2. **Value Propositions:** What problem do we solve, or which need do we meet, for each segment?
3. **Channels:** How do we reach them, and how do they want to be reached?
4. **Customer Relationships:** What relationship does each segment expect: self-service, personal help, community?
5. **Revenue Streams:** What are customers willing to pay for, and how?
6. **Key Resources:** What do we need to have: people, technology, brand, data?
7. **Key Activities:** What must we do well to deliver the value proposition?
8. **Key Partnerships:** Who helps us: suppliers, platforms, partners?
9. **Cost Structure:** What are the biggest costs, and which blocks drive them?

Then mark the **three riskiest assumptions** with a red dot and write how you could test each one.

# ⚡ Tips

- One idea per sticky note. Short phrases, not sentences.
- Fill the right side first. If you can't name the customer and the value, the left side doesn't matter.
- Use different colours for different customer segments, and match them across the blocks.
- It's a hypothesis, not a plan. Expect to redraw it.

# 📝 Summary

The **Business Model Canvas** puts a whole business on one page: who it serves, what it offers, how it reaches them, how it earns, and what it costs. Filling it in forces you to connect user needs to a sustainable product, and to see which business assumptions are risky enough to test.
