# Service Design Blueprint

# 🪜 Stage: Define

From the customer's journey to everything that makes it happen behind the scenes.

# 👋 Context

Your journey map shows the experience from the customer's side. But every step a customer takes is held up by people, tools and processes they never see: the kitchen behind the counter, the warehouse behind the "Order shipped" email.

A **service blueprint**, in Nielsen Norman Group's definition, is a diagram that shows how **people, props and processes** relate to each other at the touchpoints of one customer journey. It is the other half of the journey map: where the journey map asks "what does the customer go through?", the blueprint asks "what does the organisation have to do to make that happen?"

Blueprints are how service designers find the real root of a bad experience. Very often, the problem the customer feels on the surface (waiting, repeating themselves, being passed around) is caused by something broken backstage.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- Understand the five key elements of a service blueprint and the three lines that separate them.
- Map one customer journey from the customer's actions down to the support processes behind it.
- Find pain points, gaps and redundancies that sit below the line of visibility.
- Link a customer-facing problem to its backstage cause.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

Work in **FigJam** or **Miro**, starting from your As-Is journey map.

1. **Pick one journey and one goal.** For example "Ordering a coffee to take away" with the goal "cut the waiting time".
2. **Customer actions (top lane).** Copy the steps from your journey map, left to right.
3. **Frontstage actions.** Under each step, write what the customer sees and interacts with: staff, screens, kiosks, the app.
4. **Draw the line of visibility.** Everything below it is invisible to the customer.
5. **Backstage actions.** What staff or systems do out of sight to support each frontstage step.
6. **Support processes (bottom lane).** The internal processes, suppliers and systems that make it all possible: stock, payments, rota, CRM.
7. **Evidence (above the customer).** The props and places the customer touches at each step: signage, receipts, emails, the cup.
8. **Connect with arrows.** Show which steps depend on which. Mark pain points in red.
9. **Spot the gaps.** Circle the moments where something is repeated, slow, missing or handed over between teams.

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- ~1.5 hours

# 🏞️ Template

Lanes, from top to bottom:

- 🧾 **Evidence**: props and places the customer touches
- 🙋 **Customer actions**
- — *Line of interaction* —
- 🧑‍🍳 **Frontstage actions**: what the customer sees
- — *Line of visibility* —
- ⚙️ **Backstage actions**: what happens out of sight
- — *Line of internal interaction* —
- 🏗️ **Support processes**: systems, suppliers, policies

# ⚡ Tips

- One blueprint per scenario. "Take away" and "eat in" are two different blueprints.
- Start from research, not guesswork. Talk to staff if you can: they know the backstage.
- Keep it rough at first. Sticky notes are fine; the value is in the conversation.
- Add time, emotions or metrics only if they help you answer your goal.

# 📝 Summary

A **service blueprint** extends the journey map below the surface. By laying out customer actions, frontstage and backstage actions, support processes and evidence on one diagram, you can see how the organisation delivers an experience, and where it breaks. It is the tool that turns "the customer is frustrated" into "this is why, and this is what to fix".
