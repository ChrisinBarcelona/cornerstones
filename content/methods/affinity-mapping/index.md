# Affinity Mapping

# 🪜 Stage: Define

From a wall of sticky notes to a handful of clear themes.

# 👋 Context

After interviews, surveys, site visits and tests you have a lot of raw data: quotes, observations, numbers, surprises. On its own, none of it tells you what to do.

**Affinity mapping** (also called affinity diagramming) is how you make sense of it. Nielsen Norman Group describes it as organising related observations, ideas or findings into distinct **clusters**. You write one finding per sticky note, then group the notes by what they have in common, and name each group. The names of those groups become your **themes**, and your themes become the insights that drive the design.

It works best as a team: sorting the notes together is how a team reaches a shared understanding of the research.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- Turn raw research data into clusters and named themes.
- Practise building a shared understanding of research as a team.
- Prioritise the themes that matter most for the next design step.
- Agree on actions and owners for the top themes.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

Work in **FigJam** or **Miro** (or on a real wall).

1. **Generate the notes (10 min).** Each person writes findings from their research on sticky notes: **one observation per note**, in plain language, with the participant number (e.g. "P3 – checks the bus app three times before leaving"). Work silently, on your own.
2. **Cluster (20–30 min).** Put all the notes up. Move notes that belong together next to each other. Don't name the groups yet; let them form from the content.
3. **Name the clusters (10 min).** Give each cluster a short label that says what it means, e.g. "People don't trust the arrival times". If a note fits two clusters, duplicate it.
4. **Prioritise (10 min).** Dot-vote: each person gets three dots for the clusters that matter most to the user and the problem.
5. **Decide next steps (10 min).** For the top clusters, write an action (more research, a design idea, a fix) and who owns it. Photograph or export the board.

# ⏱️ Estimated Duration

- ~1 hour

# 🏞️ Template

- 🟨 Raw notes (one finding each, with participant number)
- 🟦 Cluster labels (written as a sentence)
- 🔴 Dot votes
- 🟩 Actions and owners

# ⚡ Tips

- Filter first. Bring the strongest findings, not every word of every transcript.
- Don't force clusters. Some will be big, some small; a small cluster can still be important.
- Don't throw out the outliers. One odd note can be the start of a big insight.
- Watch out for the loudest voice in the room. Sort in silence first, then discuss.
- Label clusters as findings ("Users plan the night before"), not topics ("Planning").

# 📝 Summary

**Affinity mapping** turns a pile of research into a few clear themes. By writing one finding per note, sorting them together and naming the groups, a team builds a shared understanding of what the research says, and decides together what matters most.
