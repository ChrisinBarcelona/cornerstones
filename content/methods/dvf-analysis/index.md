# DVF Analysis

# 🪜 Stage: Define

Desirable, viable, feasible: which ideas are worth building first?

# 👋 Context

By now you have more ideas and features than you can possibly build. You need a fair way to choose.

The **Desirability, Viability, Feasibility (DVF)** scorecard was developed by IDEO in the early 2000s and is still widely used across the industry. Nielsen Norman Group describes it as a prioritisation method that scores each idea against three criteria and adds up the totals:

- **Desirability:** Do users actually want or need this? What unique value does it give them?
- **Viability:** Is it good for the business? What does it cost, and is it sustainable over time?
- **Feasibility:** Can we build it? Do the skills, time and technology exist?

The best ideas sit where all three overlap. The score is a starting point for discussion, not the final answer.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- Score a list of ideas or features for desirability, viability and feasibility.
- Back each score with evidence from research, the business model or the team.
- Rank ideas and discuss the results as a team.
- Agree which ideas go into the next prototype.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

1. **Answer the DVF questions.** Before you score anything, answer these questions about your product as a team, in writing. Mark every answer you can back with research, and every one that's still a guess.

   **Desirability: do they want this?**
   - Who are the target customers for our solution?
   - What problem do our customers want solved?
   - How do our customers solve this problem today?
   - What is the outcome our customers want to achieve?
   - Why will our customers stop using their current solution?

   **Viability: should we do this?**
   - What are our main channels for obtaining new customers?
   - How will our customers repeatedly use our solution?
   - Why will our customers refer us to new clients?
   - Who are the primary competitors to our solution?
   - How will our solution generate revenue?

   **Feasibility: can we do this?**
   - What are our biggest technical challenges?
   - What are our biggest legal or regulatory risks?
   - Does our team have the right skills to support this solution?
   - Where does our funding come from?

2. **List the ideas.** Take 5–10 ideas or features from your ideation or affinity map. Write each as a short phrase.
3. **Build the scorecard.** A table with one row per idea and columns for **Desirability**, **Viability**, **Feasibility** and **Total**.
4. **Score 1–10.** For each idea, score each criterion and write one line of evidence for the score, using your answers to the questions above.
5. **Add up and rank.** Sort from highest to lowest total.
6. **Discuss and adjust.** Does the ranking feel right? Move items if the team can justify it. If one criterion matters more for your project, weight it (e.g. desirability ×2).
7. **Decide.** Choose the top ideas to take into your prototype, and note which ones you are parking.

# ⚡ Tips

- Score as informed as possible. Bring your research, your Business Model Canvas and someone who knows the tech.
- Score each criterion across all ideas before moving to the next. It keeps the scale consistent.
- A low feasibility score isn't always a "no": maybe it's "not yet".
- Don't let the maths decide for you. The conversation is the point.

# 📝 Summary

**DVF analysis** helps a team choose between ideas fairly. Scoring each idea for what users want, what the business needs and what can be built, then discussing the ranking, turns a long wish list into a short, defensible plan.
