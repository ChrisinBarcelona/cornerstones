# DVF Analysis

# 🪜 Stage: Define

Desirable, feasible, viable: test your idea against three lenses to find the sweet spot.

# 👋 Context

By now you have an idea, or a few, that you want to take forward. Before you invest in it, check it from every side.

You can look at any idea through **three lenses** to find the **innovation sweet spot**: where people want it, you can build it and it makes financial sense. This is the **Desirability, Feasibility, Viability (DVF)** framework, developed by IDEO as part of design thinking and still widely used across the industry.

1. **Desirable: do people want it?** The product needs to solve a real problem or meet a genuine need.
2. **Feasible: can we build it?** We must have the technical know-how to bring it to market.
3. **Viable: should we build it?** The product must contribute to our long-term success and build an advantage.

Great innovation sits where all three overlap. Where only two meet, something is missing: people want it and you can build it, but it won’t pay; people want it and it would pay, but you can’t build it; or you can build it and it would pay, but nobody wants it. Answering the questions for each lens shows you where your idea sits today.

# 🎯 Goal / Learning Outcome

- Look at an idea through the three lenses: desirable, feasible and viable.
- Answer the questions for each lens as a team.
- Back each answer with evidence, or mark it as a guess to test.
- See which lens is weakest and what you need to find out next.

# 🛠️ Main Exercise

1. **Write down the idea.** One or two sentences: what it is and who it’s for.
2. **Answer the Desirable questions.** *The product needs to solve a real problem or meet a genuine need.*

    - Who is our **target user**?
    - What is the **core problem** we are trying to solve for them?
    - How are our users **solving this problem today**?
    - Is this a problem they actively **care** about solving?
    - Why is our solution **better**?
    - Are users willing to change their **behaviour** to adopt ours?

3. **Answer the Feasible questions.** *We must have the technical know-how to bring it to market.*

    - Do we have the tech required to **build** this? If not, can we **acquire** or **develop** it?
    - Does our team have the necessary **skills**?
    - How much **time** will it take to make it viable?
    - What are the **operational** needs for maintaining this product?
    - Can we **scale** the tech reliably as the user base grows?

4. **Answer the Viable questions.** *The product must contribute to our long-term success and build an advantage.*

    - Does this product **align** with our strategy?
    - What is our **monetisation** model? (e.g. subscription, one-time purchase, freemium)
    - Are users **willing to pay** for this solution?
    - What are the **economics**? (e.g. profit, ROI, customer lifetime value)
    - How does this **position** us in the market?

5. **Mark your evidence.** Next to every answer, note whether it comes from research (interviews, surveys, desk research, the business model) or is still a guess.
6. **Find your spot on the diagram.** Which lens has the most guesses or the weakest answers? Those open questions are what you need to research or test next.

# ⚡ Tips

- Start with desirability. If nobody wants it, the other two lenses don’t matter.
- Take “how are users solving this today?” seriously: their current workaround is what your idea has to beat.
- Bring your research, someone who knows the tech and your Business Model Canvas.
- An idea that only works through one lens isn’t ready: desirable but not viable is a wish, feasible but not desirable is a gadget.
- A weak feasibility answer isn’t always a “no”: maybe it’s “not yet”.
- Be honest about guesses. They’re your next research questions.

# 📝 Summary

**DVF analysis** looks at an idea through three lenses: is it desirable (do people want it?), feasible (can we build it?) and viable (should we build it?). Great innovation sits in the sweet spot where all three meet. Answering the questions for each lens, and being honest about which answers are still guesses, shows where your idea stands and what to find out next.
