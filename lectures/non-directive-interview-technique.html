Non-Directive Interview Technique

How to run a non-directive interview: let the participant lead and don't steer their answers.

For the interviews, a powerful technique to use is the non-interventional or non-directive interviewing method.

This is arguably the most challenging and yet often this most rewarding style of interview technique we as UX Designers can implement. In a nut shell. We do not directly intervene in the interaction that the test subject has with the prototype.

non-directive interviewing is a qualitative research method that aims to gather rich and in-depth information from participants by allowing them to freely express their thoughts, experiences, and perspectives without any predetermined structure or specific questioning. The interviewer adopts a more passive role, known as a facilitator.

In a non-directive interview, the facilitator typically provides an open-ended question or topic, and then allows the participant to guide the discussion. The facilitator avoids leading questions or imposing their own biases or assumptions on the participant's responses. The focus is on the participant's subjective experience and their interpretation of the topic being explored. The facilitator listens actively, encourages elaboration, and avoids interrupting or redirecting the conversation unless necessary to maintain focus.

Non-directive interviewing is often used in qualitative research studies, where the aim is to gain a deep understanding of participants' perspectives, motivations, and lived experiences.

By allowing participants to freely express themselves, non-directive interviews can reveal rich insights and provide a more nuanced understanding of the research topic.

With non interventional interview techniques, we want to avoid specifics. We don’t want to tell people what we are looking for. We are not trying to intervene in the interview. We simply give them the objective and ask them to speak aloud.

Even phrases like,

“what did you think of the colour?”

is a type of bias because if you didn’t bring up the subject of colour, they wouldn’t have commented on it at this moment. The subject should be revealing issues which they think are relevant and if they don’t reveal anything at all, don’t force them to. Even silence is data science.

  • Let the interview environment become its own reality.

If and / or when a subject says something which touches upon the subject you are researching. Rather than steer, introduce a follow up question.

“Maybe, you could expand on that.”

Because as a facilitator, using the non-directive approach, you should never try to drive an interview towards a conclusion, or ask a subject to answer a specific question during a session.

  • Do not have any expectations.

Often it is the case that we try and survive the interview or keep it on track. If the interview fails, let it fail, this is good data.

  • If the subject corners you, give an open ended response;

“How do you think you might proceed?”

It’s like a game of Cluedo where we are not aloud to give the answer. We are only aloud to give a clue.

Good task instructions are like clues in a board game. it’s no fun (and not useful) if you’re told where to go and what to do.

If you answer a direct question from a subject during a usability session or interview, you have probably fallen victim to a type of bias known as the researcher bias.

  • Stay Neutral

Researcher bias (or experimenter bias) refers to the tendency of a researcher or an interviewer to introduce their own beliefs, opinions, or biases into the research process, potentially influencing the participants' responses or perceptions.

By answering a direct question from the subject, you risk introducing your own bias into the session, which can impact the objectivity and validity of the data you collect.

Task

  • Identify 5 people who, best case, fit the demographic you are building your product for
  • Arrange a suitable time and data for a prototype testing session
  • Conduct interview
  • Record interview
  • Ask someone to take photos of the session
  • Playback the interview and identify key quotes
  • Repeat five times
