A mental model is what someone believes about how a system works, built from everything they've experienced before.

UX Deck 10 · Mental Models2 / 12

Four features

  • Simplified A shortcut for understanding something complex
  • Influential It shapes what we notice, decide and do
  • Experience-based Built from past experience, culture and learning
  • Evolving It updates as we learn something new

A model you can touch

Want to tilt the seat back? Tilt the seat-shaped button back.

The control matches your model of the thing it controls. No label needed.

UX Deck 10 · Mental Models4 / 12

Biases are flawed models

Biases often come from mental models that are incomplete, outdated or too simple.

If someone's model of leadership is masculine, they may struggle to see women as effective leaders.

UX Deck 10 · Mental Models5 / 12

Confirmation bias

We favour information that confirms what we already believe, and ignore what doesn't.

Our mental models filter what we notice. In research, that's dangerous: you'll hear what you expected to hear.

Designing with models

Use what people already know.

Recognition rather than recall

When people recognise a button, an icon or a menu, they use the model they already have.

Recognising is faster and takes less effort than remembering. People plan their next move based on what they expect to happen.

UX Deck 10 · Mental Models8 / 12

Users spend most of their time on other sites.

Jakob's Law, from Jakob Nielsen. They expect yours to work the same way.

UX Deck 10 · Mental Models9 / 12

That's why consistency is so important in UI design.

Match the model, and the product feels intuitive.

UX Deck 10 · Mental Models10 / 12

Spot the broken model

  1. Clicking the logo in the top-left does nothing
  2. The basket icon sits at the bottom-left of a shop's website
  3. Turning a thermostat dial to the right makes the room colder
  4. A Save button uses a floppy-disk icon
  5. On a banking app, red numbers mean money coming in

Which ones break a model? Where did that model come from?

UX Deck 10 · Mental Models11 / 12

Check yourself

  1. Broken. Almost every website takes you home from the logo (Jakob's Law)
  2. Broken. Shoppers look top-right, because most shops put it there
  3. Broken. Right means more on dials and volume knobs everywhere
  4. Not broken. Few users have seen a floppy disk, but the icon has become its own learned model
  5. Broken. In banking, red means money going out or a negative balance
