The Toyota Way

Lean thinking from a car factory.

UX Deck · Agile, Lean and the Toyota Way5 / 14

Small and smart

After the Second World War, Toyota couldn't compete with American car makers on size or money.

Engineers such as Taiichi Ohno built a different system: the Toyota Production System, known worldwide as lean manufacturing.

Build what customers need, when they need it, and keep removing waste.

UX Deck · Agile, Lean and the Toyota Way6 / 14

Lean ideas designers still use

  • Continuous flow Small batches moving steadily, not big launches
  • Andon Anyone can pull the cord to stop the line when something's wrong
  • Heijunka An even, sustainable pace of work
  • Visual control Everyone can see the state of the work
  • Respect for people Those doing the work know best how to improve it
  • Kaizen Continuous improvement, every day

The Agile Manifesto

Snowbird, Utah, 2001.

UX Deck · Agile, Lean and the Toyota Way8 / 14

Seventeen frustrated developers

In 2001, seventeen software developers met at a ski lodge in Snowbird, Utah.

They were tired of slow, document-heavy projects, and wrote the Manifesto for Agile Software Development.

UX Deck · Agile, Lean and the Toyota Way9 / 14

Four values

  • Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
  • Working software over comprehensive documentation
  • Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
  • Responding to change over following a plan

At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective.

Lean or not?

Which lean idea or Agile value does each team need?

  1. Three weeks polishing hi-fi screens before anyone tests them
  2. Nobody knows who's doing what; tasks live in people's heads
  3. A designer spots a broken flow mid-sprint but stays quiet to hit the deadline
  4. A test changes the brief; the manager says "it's not in the contract"
  5. Six months to design a simple landing page
  6. The sprint ends and the team moves straight on
UX Deck · Agile, Lean and the Toyota Way13 / 14

Check yourself

  1. Working software over documentation and small batches: test something rough early
  2. Visual control: put it on a board with To do, Doing and Done
  3. Andon: stop and fix it now, while it's cheap
  4. Customer collaboration and responding to change
  5. Parkinson's Law: time-box it to a week
  6. Kaizen and the retrospective: reflect, then improve the next sprint
