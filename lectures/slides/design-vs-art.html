Not Balenciaga

A couture gown can be breathtaking, impractical and made for one person.

That's art. Your work has to make sense to people who aren't you, who are in a hurry, and who will never read your design rationale.

Two traditions

What art history can teach a designer.

UX Deck · Critical Thinking over Artistry4 / 11

Classicism

Ancient Greek and Roman principles: form, simplicity, proportion and clear structure, with restrained emotion and an appeal to the intellect.

Like design thinking, it's preoccupied with structure and strategy.

UX Deck · Critical Thinking over Artistry5 / 11

Romanticism and abstraction

Emotion, imagination and the individual come first.

Kandinsky's abstract work uses shape, colour and line almost independently of the visible world.

UX Deck · Critical Thinking over Artistry6 / 11

Both are valuable.

But a design system, a checkout or a hospital app needs the discipline of the first more than the freedom of the second.

UX Deck · Critical Thinking over Artistry8 / 11

Applicability, not allegory

J.R.R. Tolkien preferred applicability, which lives in the freedom of the reader, to allegory, where the author decides the meaning.

Good UX works the same way: the user, not the designer, decides what the product means to them.
