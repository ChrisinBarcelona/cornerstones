Kahneman

In 2002, the psychologist Daniel Kahneman won the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences.

His work, much of it with Amos Tversky, showed how people make judgements under uncertainty, and helped found behavioural economics.

We are not perfectly rational.

Our minds use two modes of thinking: System 1 and System 2.

System 1: try these

  1. Complete the phrase: bread and …
  2. What's 2 + 2?
  3. What's the first letter of the word "Bing"?

System 1 is fast

  • Automatic It works with little or no effort
  • Pattern-matching It recognises, recalls and jumps to conclusions
  • Biased Quick, but prone to error

  1. What's 17 × 24?
  2. Tell me about the last time you heard a surprising sound.
  3. How many i's are in "indivisibilities"?

System 2 is slow

For your users: design for System 1

Most of the time, people use products on autopilot.

Familiar patterns, clear hierarchy and low effort let them act without thinking hard. Don't make users think.

But when a decision really matters, a payment, a deletion, sharing personal data, slow them down and let System 2 check it.

UX Deck · Fast and Slow Thinking9 / 13

For you: use System 2

Designers fall for System 1 too: first ideas, gut feelings, "it looks good".

Don't rush into Figma. Question your assumptions, and let research and testing lead.

The same knowledge can be misused: keeping people on autopilot so they agree to things they'd refuse if they stopped to think.

Fast or slow?

Is each step System 1 or System 2? Is that right for the moment?

  1. Tapping a familiar ☰ menu icon
  2. Comparing three insurance plans with different excesses
  3. Typing DELETE to close your account
  4. Re-entering your card number to "confirm" it
  5. A pre-ticked box: "Add travel insurance, €19"

Check yourself

  1. System 1. Right: it's a familiar pattern
  2. System 2. Unavoidable, so help it: a clear table and a recommended option
  3. System 2. Right: a deliberate pause before something you can't undo
  4. System 2. Wrong: effort with no benefit. Remove it
  5. System 1, exploited. People on autopilot pay for something they didn't choose. That's a deceptive pattern
