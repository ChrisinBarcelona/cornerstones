The halo effect

"Beauty" is subjective and shaped by culture. What researchers can measure is how people are rated.

People rated as more attractive tend to be judged more favourably on unrelated qualities: competence, honesty, intelligence.

UX Deck · The Bias of Beauty4 / 12

What the research found

  • Judgements A review of more than 900 studies (Langlois and colleagues, 2000): people rated attractive are judged and treated more positively
  • Earnings Economists Hamermesh and Biddle (1994) found a measurable pay gap linked to rated attractiveness
  • Not fair It says more about our biases than about people

Beauty buys patience

Users judge beautiful interfaces as easier to use and more trustworthy, even when they aren't. That's the aesthetic-usability effect.

Good visual design buys you trust and patience.

But beauty can also hide usability problems. Test, don't trust first impressions.

Heuristic 8: aesthetic and minimalist design

Interfaces should only include what's necessary and high in value.

Every extra piece of information competes with the important ones, and makes them harder to see.

Clarity always wins over visual flourish.

Form follows function.

Louis Sullivan, architect. Beauty earns initial trust; without substance, users soon leave.

UX Deck · The Bias of Beauty10 / 12

Does it earn its place?

A product page for a reusable water bottle has:

Product photo · Name · Price · Add to basket · Star rating · Size and colour pickers · Animated sparkles around the bottle · A 300-word brand story · Six social share icons · A countdown timer to "the end of the sale", which resets daily

Keep, move or cut each one?

UX Deck · The Bias of Beauty11 / 12

Check yourself

  • Keep: photo, name, price, Add to basket, rating, size and colour. They answer what, how much and is it good
  • Move: the brand story, below the fold or to its own page; one Share button instead of six icons
  • Cut: the sparkles, which compete with the product
  • Cut, definitely: the countdown that resets daily. Beauty must never hide a bad deal: it's fake scarcity, and it costs trust
