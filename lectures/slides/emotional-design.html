Three levels

From Don Norman's Emotional Design (2004).

UX Deck · Emotional Design3 / 11

How we respond to design

  • Visceral The instant gut reaction to how it looks, sounds and feels. "Ooh, that's beautiful."
  • Behavioural How it feels to use: easy, efficient, in control. "That was so simple."
  • Reflective What it means afterwards: memories, self-image, the story we tell. "This is the kind of product I use."
UX Deck · Emotional Design4 / 11

Good design works on all three.

Beautiful but frustrating fails at the behavioural level. Usable but embarrassing fails at the reflective one.

UX Deck · Emotional Design5 / 11

We decide with feeling

People decide emotionally far more often than they think.

Most everyday decisions are quick, intuitive and driven by feeling. The reasons come afterwards.

So the feeling a product creates decides whether people trust it, enjoy it and come back.

UX Deck · Emotional Design6 / 11

Colour

Red is energetic and grabs attention. Yellow is warm, cheerful and visible from far away.

Together they suit a brand built on speed, fun and quick decisions.

Colour meanings vary by culture: check yours.

UX Deck · Emotional Design7 / 11

Type

Type sets the tone before anyone reads a word.

Apple's own typeface, San Francisco, is clean, modern and highly legible, like the brand. Used everywhere, it makes every Apple screen feel like one family.

UX Deck · Emotional Design8 / 11

Brand

A brand is a product's personality: logo, colour, type, imagery and words.

Carolyn Davidson designed Nike's swoosh in 1971. It suggests movement and speed. With "Just Do It", it's a brand people feel something about.

Which level?

  1. "I bought it because the box was gorgeous."
  2. "Booking took ten seconds. Done."
  3. "I'd never be seen with that brand."
  4. "It froze twice, so I deleted it."
  5. "The colours make me feel calm."
  6. "I tell everyone about it."

Then: a children's dentist wants to feel calm and safe. Which colours and type would you try?
