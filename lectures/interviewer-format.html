Interviewer Format

The step-by-step structure for running a prototype interview, from the welcome to the debrief.

  • Begin by welcoming the subject into the room.
  • Invite the subject to open up about themselves.
  • Take the time to actively listen to them and identify the context of the user.
  • Introduce the prototype.
  • Give them the Objective.
  • Ask them to communicate their thought process.
  • Upon completion ask about the subject’s impression.

Welcoming the Subject

People need to feel comfortable to be open, honest, and critical. So the first job of the Interviewer is to welcome the subject and put them at their ease. That means a warm greeting and friendly small talk about the weather. It also means smiling a lot and lots of engagement. Keep everything informal. Stay neutral. What we must emphasis during the welcome is the fact that we are not testing the subject. Many subjects can some how interpret a prototype testing session as if it is a test of their abilities and we must be aware of that and avoid it.

Thanks for coming in today! Really appreciate your help here. What we have been doing over the past week is building a prototype ... etc etc. And as you understand, getting actual user feedback is a super important part of that. “This interview will be pretty informal. I’m not testing you – I’m actually testing the prototype. You know? If you get stuck or confused, it’s nothing to do with you. In fact, it actually helps us find problems we need to fix. So, yeah, let's just have a chat ... what are you into?

Invite Them to Open Up

Just listen. Be interested. Care. Build rapport. Watch your breathing. Usually UX Designers are naturally fascinated by people so simply show an interest and ask the subject to open up about themselves.

Watch out for audio and visual queues. Try to encourage a platform of equality. Make them feel as if they are equally in control.

Ask questions about their job, aspirations. What do they like and or dislike. Find common ground. One student actually hugged a subject once which was a pretty great method.

Discover the Context of the User

Start slow and begin to introduce questions which are a little more relevant to the product. If you do this correctly, the transition will seem effortless. Watch the body language. Check your timing, the subject will barely notice you have already begun the interview. This is about cadence and avoid jumping in. Try to get a sense of when a conversation is ready to naturally evolve. This is an opportunity for you to find out

  • When is the User.
  • Have they come into contact with the product before.
  • Do they like it.
  • Are they confused.
  • Do they suffer from any pain points.

Introduce the Prototype

Once you feel you have adequately contextualised the user in regard to the product, introduce the prototype.

What we are trying to achieve here is a sense of neutrality. We are trying to remove a sense of role playing that you are an interviewer and they are a test subject.

Be prepared for the fact that some people don’t know what a prototype is: in this instance, you can tell them directly that it is a mock up of an application that you are building but it isn’t a real product and therefore some buttons won’t work.

Sometimes it is helpful to ask the subject’s permission to show them the prototype.

“Do you mind if we take a look at the prototype now?”

In order to achieve a sense of neutrality, I recommend distancing yourself from it.

“Listen I didn’t actually build this, so you won’t hurt my feelings if you say anything negative.”

This type of approach helps to remove agreeableness bias which is a tendency for people to prioritise harmony in a social setting over their actual feelings.

Give Them The Objective

You may change the objective at a later point.

This is a simple supposition statement:

  • Imagine you want to (action) … in order to (goal) … so that (benefit) … how would you proceed?

The subject may immediately ask you specifics. Help them understand the test but don’t show them. If they ask where to start, stay neutral.

“Start wherever feels natural to you … ”

Ask them to share their thought process out loud

The most effective user feedback is shared by a subject plainly, openly and honestly sharing about their impressions step by step,

And so you must ask them to think out loud.

As a facilitator, it is something of a prerequisite task to get them into a frame of mind to do this. This is your job and only job. Your are a facilitator.

  • You should be actively listening and they should be speaking.

If they are not being particularly vocal, prompt them gently by asking them open ended questions;

  1. "What are your thoughts on that?"
  2. "How did you feel about that outcome?"
  3. "Tell me more about your experience there."
  4. "What are your reasons for choosing that option?"
  5. "Can you explain your perspective on the matter?"

If they get stuck, don’t give them answers.

De-Brief: Their Impression

The beauty of qualitative research methods such as prototype testing sessions are that, unlike metrics, they reveal why someone feels a certain way towards a certain product or service. A statistic can never do this.

Essentially, that is what the facilitator is trying to discover - The why of it all.

During the summative part of the interview, capture their final thoughts and conclusions. A good statement to use if necessary is the following;

“If you had three magic wishes to improve this, what would they be?”

When the interview complete, thank the subject, and show them the door.
