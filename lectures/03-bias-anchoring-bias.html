Anchoring bias is a cognitive bias that refers to the tendency for individuals to rely too heavily on the first piece of information they receive (the "anchor") when making decisions or judgments.

Anchoring Bias occurs because people tend to anchor their judgments or estimates around that initial information, even if it is arbitrary or irrelevant.

Once an anchor is established, subsequent judgments or estimates are often adjusted from that initial point, but people typically do not adjust enough to reach an unbiased conclusion. This bias can affect various aspects of decision-making, such as negotiations, pricing, or assessments of probabilities.

For example, in a negotiation, if the seller starts with a high asking price, it can act as an anchor for the buyer's perception of the item's value. Even if the buyer knows the asking price is inflated, their final offer is likely to be influenced by that initial anchor.

Anchoring bias can lead to errors in judgment and decision-making, as it can cause people to rely too heavily on limited or irrelevant information, rather than considering a broader range of factors. Being aware of this bias can help individuals make more rational and objective decisions by consciously questioning and reevaluating the influence of anchors on their judgments.
