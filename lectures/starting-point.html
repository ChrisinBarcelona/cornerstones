Start here. Or here. Or here … or here.

Anyone who has worked in any business capacity will know that it isn’t the case that projects begin as a simple nice little package of requirements and when built everything works perfectly.

In reality, when we are in the process of inception, it is a mystifying place. The structure of solution remains in the abstract with only fragments being visible at any one time. This quirk of reality is intrinsically connected to knowledge, the lack of knowledge, the lack of mastery of subject and ultimately ignorance of a subject. When we are lucky, we are handed a straightforward problem with an obvious solution. For instance.

”A hotel chain booking system requires an update to facilitate new branches in Jakarta, Bali and Surabaya.”

Updating the booking system to to incorporate all languages spoken in Indonesia would be a thoughtful conclusion arrived at without having to work terribly hard. It is not however the case that we are frequently met with little neat parcels of solutions. Market growth comes in many forms. Sometimes we are uniquely positioned to do something well. The market may or may not require it and therefore we need to go into a process of discovery.

Loosely speaking, many innovations, take for example X, formerly referred to as Twitter, started in this way.

More often than not. A brief can be abstract. For example a company needs more customers. This is frequently the case. The job then becomes a question of how. Or rather how is best, quickest, most impactful and above all, cheapest.

On other occasions, a business owner or stakeholder believes in some new or existing form of technology and believes that in order for the company to make gains, this technology must be incorporated post haste. In this example, you may find yourself working in the full sight of the fact that their hypothesis is completely incorrect.

What is important to understand here, when you begin your projects, do not assume that the way to approach the initial issue will be the same every time. In the successful practise of user experience, be prepared for your discovery phase to shift according to the manifold requirements and realities of the context of the issue at hand.
