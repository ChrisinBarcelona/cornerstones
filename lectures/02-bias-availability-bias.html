In psychology, the availability bias is the human tendency to rely on information that comes readily to mind when evaluating situations or making decisions. Because of this bias, people believe that the readily available information is more representative of fact than is the case.

How it works

For example, after seeing news of a plane crash, many people feel flying is more dangerous than driving, even though the statistics say the opposite. The crash is vivid and easy to recall; the thousands of safe flights are not.

Why it matters for UX

Users judge how likely or important something is by how easily examples come to mind, which is why recent, vivid or repeated information feels true. As a designer, the last user you spoke to or the loudest complaint can feel like the whole picture. Balance vivid stories with data from many users.

Try it: spot it in the wild (20 minutes)

  1. Write down the three biggest problems you think your product's users have.
  2. Next to each, note where that belief came from: one story, the news, or data from many users.
  3. For any that came from one vivid story, plan how you would check it with more users.

Key takeaways

  • We judge by what comes to mind easily, not by what is most common.
  • One vivid user story is not the whole picture.
  • Balance memorable anecdotes with data from many users.
